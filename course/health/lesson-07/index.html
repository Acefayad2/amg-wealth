Explanations & examples

01

Credit reports and the applicant’s rights

When Rosa applies, the insurer may order an investigative consumer report about her character, reputation and lifestyle. The Fair Credit Reporting Act requires that she be told, within three days of the request, that such a report may be made, and that she may ask about its nature and scope. If the insurer takes adverse action based on a report, such as declining or rating her, it must tell her and identify the reporting agency so she can obtain the report and dispute errors. Producers who collect applications must know these notices exist and never discourage an applicant from exercising them.

Report requested

Notice to applicant within 3 days

Applicant asks

Nature and scope disclosed

Adverse action

Notice naming the reporting agency

02

Federal insurance fraud statutes

Federal law treats insurance fraud affecting interstate commerce as a crime. Under 18 USC 1033, anyone convicted of a felony involving dishonesty or breach of trust may not work in the business of insurance without the written consent of the state insurance regulator. The same statute punishes false statements to regulators, embezzlement of insurer funds and threats against regulators or examiners, with prison terms that grow when the conduct jeopardizes an insurer’s safety. Section 1034 lets the Attorney General seek civil penalties and injunctions. For Theo the practical rule is simple: a disqualifying conviction requires a consent letter before any insurance work.

Prohibited person

Felony of dishonesty; needs written regulator consent

Prohibited acts

False statements, embezzlement, threats

Section 1034

Civil penalties and injunctions

03

ERISA: who it covers and what it demands

The Employee Retirement Income Security Act governs employee benefit plans sponsored by private employers, including group health and disability plans. Government and church plans are generally exempt. Anyone who controls plan assets or administration is a fiduciary and must act solely in the interest of participants, with the care of a prudent person, following the plan documents. Plans must report to the Department of Labor, usually on Form 5500, and must disclose terms to participants through a summary plan description delivered within 90 days of joining, plus summaries of material changes. When Priya offers a group plan, ERISA shapes her duties as sponsor.

Applies to

Private employer welfare plans

Fiduciary duty

Solely for participants; prudent person

Reporting and disclosure

Form 5500; summary plan description within 90 days

04

COBRA continuation

COBRA applies to employers with 20 or more employees on a typical business day in the prior year. A worker who loses coverage through termination, other than gross misconduct, or reduced hours may continue the group plan for 18 months, extended to 29 months if disabled within the first 60 days. Death of the employee, divorce, Medicare entitlement or a child aging out gives dependents 36 months. The employer notifies the plan administrator within 30 days, the administrator notifies the beneficiary within 14 days, and the beneficiary has 60 days to elect. The premium may reach 102 percent of the group cost.

18 months

Termination or reduced hours

29 months

Disability extension

36 months

Death, divorce, Medicare, child ages out

60 days

Election window; premium up to 102%

05

Affordable Care Act market reforms

The ACA rewrote the rules for comprehensive medical coverage. Individual health insurance coverage means coverage sold to individuals in the individual market, as opposed to group plans. Plans must cover children on a parent’s policy to age 26. They may not impose lifetime dollar limits, or annual dollar limits, on essential health benefits, the ten categories every individual and small group plan must include. Premiums may vary only by age, within a 3 to 1 ratio, tobacco use up to 1.5 to 1, geographic area and family size. Group plans may not impose waiting periods longer than 90 days.

Dependents

Covered to age 26

No dollar limits

Lifetime or annual, on essential health benefits

Rating

Age 3:1, tobacco 1.5:1, area, family size

Waiting period

90 days maximum

06

The NAIC

The National Association of Insurance Commissioners is the organization of the chief insurance regulators of the states, the District of Columbia and the territories. It drafts model laws and regulations, such as the uniform health policy provisions you will study in Lesson 15, and it accredits state insurance departments. The NAIC is not a federal agency and has no power to license producers, approve policies or enforce anything. A model becomes law in Maryland only when the General Assembly or the Insurance Commissioner adopts it. When a producer says the NAIC approved a policy, that statement is false.

What the NAIC does

Model laws, coordination, accreditation

What it does not do

License, approve policies, enforce

More detail

COBRA and Maryland continuation differ

COBRA is federal and applies to larger employers. Maryland’s own continuation statutes reach group contracts of any size and give 18 months after a loss of employment not for cause, after the death of the insured, or after divorce, with a 45-day election. Lesson 38 compares them.

Example: Priya’s bakery has 20 workers, so COBRA applies; a 12-person shop down the street would rely on Maryland continuation.

Excepted benefits sit outside the ACA rules

Limited policies such as accident-only, hospital indemnity, specified disease, stand-alone dental and vision, and disability income are excepted benefits. They are not subject to the ACA’s essential health benefit, rating and dollar-limit rules, which is one reason they must disclose their limits so clearly.

Example: A cancer policy may still have a lifetime maximum; a comprehensive medical plan may not.

Learning objectives

By the end, you can…

  • Apply the Fair Credit Reporting Act and the federal insurance fraud statutes to producer conduct.
  • Describe ERISA’s reach, fiduciary duties and disclosure rules.
  • State the COBRA and Affordable Care Act market rules that appear on the exam, and explain the NAIC’s role.
