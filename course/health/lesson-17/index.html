01

Change of occupation and misstatement of age

Optional provisions may be included at the insurer’s choice. Under change of occupation, if Rosa moves from office estimating to climbing poles, a more hazardous job, benefits are reduced to what her premium would have bought at the new occupation’s rate. If she moves to a less hazardous job, premium drops and the excess is refunded from the date of change. Under misstatement of age, if she gave the wrong birth date, benefits are adjusted to what the premium would have purchased at her true age. Neither provision voids the policy; both correct the bargain.

02

Other insurance and relation of earnings

Other insurance in this company limits the total coverage one insurer will carry on a person; excess premium is refunded. Insurance with other companies addresses duplicate coverage the insurer was not told about. On expense-incurred benefits, the insurer pays only its proportionate share of the loss and refunds the excess premium. On other benefits, such as disability income, benefits are prorated. Relation of earnings to insurance prevents profit from disability: if total disability benefits exceed the insured’s earnings at the start of disability, or average earnings over the prior two years, benefits are reduced to earnings, never below 200 dollars a month.

Relation of earnings

03

Unpaid premium, cancellation, conformity and conduct

Unpaid premium lets the insurer deduct any premium due from a claim payment. Cancellation by the company permits the insurer to cancel with written notice, commonly 45 days under state rules, refunding unearned premium pro rata; cancellation does not affect a claim that began before it. Conformity with state statutes amends any provision that conflicts with the law of the state where the insured lives so that it meets the minimum the law requires. Illegal occupation and intoxicants and narcotics relieve the insurer of liability for losses from a felony or illegal occupation, or while intoxicated or under unprescribed narcotics.

04

Free look, insuring clause and consideration

Three general provisions frame every policy. The right to examine, or free look, lets the insured return the policy within a stated period after delivery, typically ten days for individual health policies and thirty days for Medicare supplement and long-term care policies in Maryland, for a full refund. The insuring clause is the insurer’s core promise: who is covered, against which perils, for what benefits. The consideration clause states what the insured gives: the premium and the statements in the application. Together they define the exchange, and the rest of the policy qualifies it.

05

The renewability ladder

Renewability tells you who controls the policy’s future. Noncancelable is the strongest: the insurer may neither cancel nor raise the premium until a stated age, often 65. Guaranteed renewable: the insurer may not cancel or refuse renewal, but may raise premiums for an entire class of insureds. Conditionally renewable: the insurer may refuse renewal only for reasons stated in the policy, such as reaching an age or leaving a job, never for health. Renewable at the option of the insurer: the insurer may refuse renewal at any premium due date. Cancelable or nonrenewable term: the insurer may cancel at any time with notice.

Noncancelable

No cancellation, no premium increase, to a stated age

Guaranteed renewable

No cancellation; class premium increases allowed

Conditionally renewable

Nonrenewal only for stated reasons

06

Interest and military suspension

Two smaller provisions finish the set. Interest on claim proceeds requires the insurer to pay interest when a claim is paid late; Maryland’s prompt payment law sets the rate, starting at 1.5 percent a month on clean claims unpaid after thirty days. The military suspension provision lets an insured called to active duty suspend the policy, with premium refunded for the suspension period, and reinstate it without evidence of insurability within a set time after discharge. Rosa should know that a noncancelable policy she buys today protects both her coverage and her price, while a guaranteed renewable medical plan protects only the coverage.

More detail

Why comprehensive medical plans are guaranteed renewable

Federal law requires individual and group comprehensive medical coverage to be guaranteed renewable, with nonrenewal allowed only for nonpayment, fraud, moving out of the service area or the insurer leaving the market. Premiums may still rise by class each year, which is the defining feature of guaranteed renewable coverage.

Example: Marcus’s individual medical plan cannot be canceled because he developed diabetes, but his premium can rise with everyone else’s in his age band and area.

Noncancelable costs more for a reason

A noncancelable disability policy locks the premium schedule, so the insurer bears the risk that claims run higher than expected. Guaranteed renewable disability coverage costs less because the insurer can adjust class premiums. Producers should present the trade-off plainly.

Example: Rosa pays about 20 percent more for a noncancelable policy whose premium cannot change until age 65.
