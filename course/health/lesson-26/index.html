01

Children to age 26

Marcus’s older daughter is 24, finished with school, working part time and living in another city. Under the Affordable Care Act she may stay on his plan until she turns 26, regardless of whether she is a student, married, financially dependent or living at home. Maryland law separately requires coverage of unmarried child dependents to age 25; the federal rule governs plans subject to it, and the more generous rule applies. When she reaches 26 she loses eligibility, which is a qualifying event for COBRA or Maryland continuation and a special enrollment opportunity on Maryland Health Connection.

Federal dependent coverage limit

Age 25

Maryland statute for unmarried child dependents

Turning 26

02

Preventive care and pre-existing conditions

Non-grandfathered plans must cover recommended preventive services with no deductible, copayment or coinsurance when delivered in network: screenings and counseling rated A or B by the U.S. Preventive Services Task Force, recommended immunizations, and the preventive care guidelines for women and children. Elena’s mammogram and the children’s vaccines cost nothing out of pocket. Plans may not deny coverage, exclude a condition or charge more because of a pre-existing condition, for adults or children. The HIPAA exclusion periods you learned in Lesson 24 are gone from comprehensive coverage.

Preventive care

No cost sharing in network

Pre-existing conditions

No denial, exclusion or surcharge

Who

All comprehensive individual and group plans

03

Rescissions and dollar limits

Before the ACA, some insurers canceled coverage retroactively when a member got sick, hunting the application for errors. Rescission is now permitted only for fraud or an intentional misrepresentation of a material fact, and the insurer must give 30 days’ advance notice so the member can appeal. Honest mistakes cannot cost coverage. Plans also may not impose lifetime dollar limits on essential health benefits, or annual dollar limits on them. A plan may still limit non-essential benefits and may use visit limits that are not dollar caps where permitted. The result is that catastrophic illness no longer exhausts a policy.

Rescission

Only for fraud or intentional misrepresentation; 30 days’ notice

Dollar limits

No lifetime or annual limits on essential health benefits

04

Grandfathered plans

A plan that existed on March 23, 2010 and has not made significant changes, such as cutting benefits, raising coinsurance, or sharply increasing deductibles or employee contributions, is grandfathered. Grandfathered plans must still cover children to 26, drop lifetime limits and stop rescissions, but they are exempt from some rules: free preventive care, essential health benefits, the new appeals process and the rating limits. Each year fewer remain. Once a plan loses grandfathered status it cannot regain it. Marcus’s employer plan lost its status when it doubled the deductible, so it now meets every ACA rule.

Grandfathered

Existed March 23, 2010 without significant change; partial exemption

Non-grandfathered

All ACA rules apply

One way

Status lost cannot be regained

05

Essential health benefits

Individual and small group plans must cover ten categories of essential health benefits. Ambulatory patient services. Emergency services. Hospitalization. Maternity and newborn care. Mental health and substance use disorder services, including behavioral health treatment. Prescription drugs. Rehabilitative and habilitative services and devices. Laboratory services. Preventive and wellness services and chronic disease management. Pediatric services, including oral and vision care. Each state picks a benchmark plan that defines the details; Maryland’s benchmark folds in the state mandates. Large group plans are not required to cover all ten but may not put dollar limits on any they do cover.

Care settings

Ambulatory, emergency, hospitalization

Life events and conditions

Maternity and newborn; mental health and substance use

Services

Drugs, rehabilitative and habilitative, laboratory, preventive and chronic disease

Children

Pediatric services including oral and vision

06

Applying the coverage rules

Run Marcus’s family through the rules. The 24-year-old stays on the plan. Preventive visits are free in network. The younger child’s asthma cannot be excluded or surcharged. If Marcus forgot to list a decade-old prescription on an application, the plan cannot rescind for an honest omission. A 400,000 dollar hospital bill cannot exhaust a lifetime maximum, because there is none on essential benefits. And because his employer’s plan is no longer grandfathered, every one of these protections applies. Lesson 27 turns to the parts of the law that shape the marketplace: metal levels, appeals, subsidies and penalties.

More detail

Excepted benefits are outside these rules

Accident-only, hospital indemnity, specified disease, dental-only, vision-only, disability income and long-term care policies are excepted benefits and are not subject to the ACA coverage rules in this lesson. That is why those policies may still underwrite, exclude pre-existing conditions and cap benefits.

Example: Rosa’s cancer policy may have a $50,000 lifetime maximum; her medical plan may not.

Habilitative versus rehabilitative

Rehabilitative services restore skills lost to injury or illness, such as physical therapy after a stroke. Habilitative services help a person acquire skills never developed, such as speech therapy for a child with a developmental delay. Both are essential health benefits.

Example: A plan that covers physical therapy after surgery must also cover speech therapy for a child’s developmental delay.
