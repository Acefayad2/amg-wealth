01

Metal levels

Every marketplace plan covers the same essential health benefits; the metal level tells you how the cost is split. Actuarial value is the share of covered costs the plan pays for a standard population. Bronze plans pay about 60 percent, silver about 70, gold about 80 and platinum about 90. Lower metals mean lower premiums and higher deductibles. Catastrophic plans, with very high deductibles, are limited to people under 30 or those with a hardship exemption. Marcus’s daughter, turning 26 and healthy, might pick bronze; Rosa, who expects a surgery, might pick gold.

02

The small business marketplace

Maryland Health Connection runs the state’s individual Exchange and a Small Business Health Options Program, or SHOP, for employers with up to 50 employees. In SHOP the employer picks a contribution level and one or more plans, employees enroll, and the employer receives a single consolidated bill and pays it; the marketplace or its administrator distributes premiums to the carriers. Enrollment may run through an authorized producer. Employers with fewer than 25 full-time equivalent employees and modest average wages may qualify for the small business health care tax credit, available only for coverage bought through SHOP, for two consecutive years.

03

Internal appeal

When a plan denies a claim, refuses to authorize care or rescinds coverage, that is an adverse benefit determination, and the member may appeal. The first step is an internal appeal to the plan, requested within 180 days of the denial. The plan must have the appeal reviewed by someone not involved in the original decision and, for medical judgments, by an appropriate clinician. Decisions are due within 30 days for care not yet received and 60 days for care already received. Urgent cases get an expedited decision; Maryland requires emergency grievance decisions within 24 hours. The member may see the file and submit evidence.

04

External review

If the plan upholds a denial based on medical necessity or similar judgment, the member may seek external review by an independent party. In Maryland, the Insurance Administration handles complaints about adverse decisions, obtains an independent medical review when needed, and its determination binds the carrier. The Attorney General’s Health Education and Advocacy Unit helps consumers file grievances and gather records. External review is free to the member and must be decided within set time limits, with expedited review for urgent care. A member may also skip the internal step when the carrier fails to follow its own process or the case is urgent.

05

Subsidies

People who buy through the individual Exchange may qualify for a premium tax credit based on household income relative to the federal poverty level. The credit is usually paid in advance directly to the insurer, lowering the monthly premium, and is reconciled on the tax return. It is not available to people offered affordable employer coverage or eligible for Medicaid; in Maryland, adults with income below 138 percent of poverty generally qualify for Medicaid instead. Cost-sharing reductions lower deductibles and copayments for enrollees between 100 and 250 percent of poverty, but only in silver plans. The income ranges and credit amounts are set by Congress and change, so check current figures.

06

Penalties and fines

The federal individual mandate penalty was reduced to zero beginning in 2019, so an individual who goes without coverage owes no federal penalty, and Maryland imposes none; Maryland instead uses an Easy Enrollment checkbox on the state tax return to connect uninsured residents with coverage. Penalties remain for employers and insurers. An applicable large employer, one with 50 or more full-time equivalent employees, that fails to offer affordable minimum value coverage to its full-time employees can owe the employer shared responsibility payment. Insurers and group plans that violate ACA rules face federal excise taxes and state fines through the Insurance Administration.

More detail

Why silver matters for cost-sharing reductions

Cost-sharing reductions attach only to silver plans. An eligible enrollee who picks bronze gets the premium credit but not the lower deductible. Producers should check income before recommending a metal level.

Example: Rosa, at 200 percent of poverty, pays less out of pocket with a silver plan than with a cheaper bronze plan once the reductions apply.

Grievance versus complaint in Maryland

A grievance is the member’s internal challenge to a carrier’s adverse decision. A complaint is the request for the Insurance Administration’s external review after the grievance fails, or sooner when the carrier does not meet its deadlines or the case is urgent.

Example: Marcus files a grievance with his carrier first; when it is denied, he files a complaint with the MIA, which orders an independent review.
