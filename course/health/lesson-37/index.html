01

Employee eligibility

Priya’s plan defines who may enroll. Employees must be in an eligible class, typically full-time workers averaging 30 or more hours a week, and must be actively at work when coverage begins. New hires usually serve a probationary, or waiting, period before becoming eligible; federal law caps it at 90 days. When the waiting period ends, the employee has an eligibility period, usually 31 days, to enroll without evidence of insurability. An employee who misses it is a late enrollee and may have to wait for the next open enrollment. Part-time employees may be included at the employer’s option under Maryland’s small group rules.

02

Dependent eligibility

Eligible dependents include the employee’s spouse and children. Federal law requires coverage of children to age 26; Maryland law separately requires coverage of unmarried child dependents to age 25 and extends dependent status to grandchildren in the insured’s court-ordered custody. Newborns are covered from birth and adopted children from placement, with notice and premium due within 31 days when an extra premium applies. Children with intellectual or physical disabilities who cannot support themselves remain covered past the age limit with proof. Children named in a medical child support order must be enrolled regardless of enrollment periods.

03

Open and special enrollment

Once a year the plan holds an annual open enrollment, when any eligible employee may join or change coverage without a qualifying event. Between open enrollments, HIPAA’s special enrollment periods allow entry after a loss of other coverage, marriage, birth, adoption or placement, if requested within 30 days, and after a change in Medicaid or children’s health program eligibility, within 60 days. Priya’s employee who waived coverage because of her husband’s plan may join within 30 days when he loses his job. Outside these windows the plan may make a late enrollee wait, though it may not exclude conditions.

04

Coordination of benefits at work

Group plans include the coordination of benefits provision from Lesson 23. For an employee covered under two group plans, the plan covering her as an employee is primary. For a dependent child, the birthday rule applies, or the custodial parent’s plan when parents are divorced without a decree. The secondary plan pays the remaining allowable expense up to its own benefit, so payments never exceed the bill. Employers ask about other coverage at enrollment because COB savings lower the group’s claims. An employee who conceals a spouse’s plan may have claims reprocessed and benefits recovered.

05

Minimizing adverse selection

Every rule above also serves a defensive purpose. Waiting periods and the actively-at-work requirement stop someone from joining the payroll to get treatment for an existing problem. Limited enrollment windows prevent people from buying coverage only when sick and dropping it when well. Participation and contribution rules keep healthy employees in the pool. Employee classes must be defined by conditions of employment, not health. Dependents enroll when the employee does, or at a qualifying event, not at the moment of a diagnosis. Together these tools let the insurer skip individual underwriting and still price the group soundly.

06

Enroll Priya’s new baker

A new baker starts March 1, full time. Her 60-day waiting period ends April 30; she has until May 31 to enroll herself, her husband and her two children without evidence of insurability. She enrolls on May 10 with coverage effective June 1. In September her husband loses his job and his coverage; she adds him within 30 days under special enrollment. In December a daughter is born and is covered from birth; she notifies the plan within 31 days and pays the added premium. Each step follows a rule in this lesson, and none required a medical question.

More detail

Actively at work

Group coverage usually begins only if the employee is actively at work on the effective date; an employee out sick or on leave is covered when she returns. Federal nondiscrimination rules prevent this clause from being used against someone absent for a health reason beyond a short delay, and plans must apply it uniformly.

Example: A new hire hospitalized on her scheduled effective date is covered on her first day back at work.

Maryland’s dependent rules in a group plan

Maryland’s requirements for newborns, adopted children, grandchildren in custody, disabled dependents, medical child support orders and dependents to age 25 all apply to group contracts issued in Maryland. Lesson 56 covers them as regulation; here they define who is eligible.

Example: A court orders an employee to cover his son; the plan must enroll the boy even though open enrollment ended months ago.
