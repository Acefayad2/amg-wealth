Explanations & examples

01

Counting to fifty

Is Priya’s bakery a small employer? Maryland defines a small employer as one that employed an average of not more than 50 employees during the preceding calendar year. The count is full-time employees, those averaging 30 or more hours a week, plus full-time equivalents, calculated each month by dividing the hours of part-time employees by 120. Seasonal workers are excluded. Priya has 14 full-time workers and six part-timers at 20 hours a week, about 480 part-time hours a month, which is four full-time equivalents. Eighteen in all: a small employer. A firm with 60 full-time workers is a large group.

02

Guaranteed availability

A carrier that sells small group coverage in Maryland must accept every small employer that applies and meets its uniformly applied participation and contribution requirements, and must enroll every eligible employee the employer offers coverage to. It may not deny, limit or condition coverage because of the health status of the employer’s workers, and discriminating against employees on the basis of health status is an unfair trade practice. The only lawful reasons to turn down a small group are failure to meet participation or contribution rules, location outside the carrier’s service area or the carrier’s lack of capacity, applied to all groups alike.

03

Disclosure and eligibility

Carriers must give small employers plain-language disclosure of the plan’s benefits, exclusions, premium rating practices, renewability terms and participation requirements before the sale, so Priya can compare offers honestly. Eligible employees are those the employer offers coverage to; the employer may limit eligibility to full-time employees or include part-time employees working at least 17.5 hours a week, but it must apply the choice to the whole class. Dependents are eligible under the rules in Lesson 37. Waiting periods may not exceed 90 days. An employee who declines may join later only at open enrollment or a special enrollment event.

04

Rating the small group

Small group premiums are set by modified community rating. For plans issued or renewed since 2014, rates may vary only by whether coverage is individual or family, by rating area, by age within a 3 to 1 ratio for adults, and by tobacco use within 1.5 to 1. Nothing else: not health, not claims, not industry, not gender. Rates are fixed for the entire plan year, so Priya’s premium cannot change midyear when an employee gets sick. Carriers must use accepted actuarial methods and apply risk adjustment consistently. Grandfathered small group plans keep an older Maryland rating structure with limited adjustments.

05

Renewability

Small group coverage is guaranteed renewable at the employer’s option. A carrier may refuse to renew only for nonpayment of premium, fraud or intentional misrepresentation, failure to meet participation or contribution requirements, the employer leaving the carrier’s service area, or the carrier’s withdrawal from the small group market with the required notice, after which it may not reenter for five years. The group’s claims, the illness of an employee or the age of the workforce are never grounds. Renewal premiums may change within the rating rules. This is HIPAA’s renewability guarantee as implemented in Maryland’s small group law.

Permitted nonrenewal

Nonpayment; fraud; participation failure; service area; market exit

Never

Claims, illness, age of employees

06

Priya’s options

As a small employer, Priya may buy directly from a carrier or through Maryland Health Connection for Small Business, the SHOP marketplace, with Theo as her authorized producer. Either way she gets guaranteed availability, community rating and guaranteed renewability. Through SHOP she may qualify for the small business health care tax credit if she has fewer than 25 full-time equivalent employees and modest average wages, and she can offer employee choice among plans. She must meet the carrier’s participation and contribution rules and may define eligibility for her part-time staff. Her employees’ health never enters the price.

More detail

Groups of one

Maryland’s statute sets no minimum size for a small employer, but group coverage requires at least one common-law employee other than the owner and the owner’s spouse. A self-employed person with no employees buys in the individual market, where coverage is also guaranteed issue.

Example: Rosa, with no employees, shops the individual Exchange; Priya, with 18, is a small group.

Grandfathered small group rating

Small group plans grandfathered under the Affordable Care Act continue under Maryland’s pre-2014 rating structure, which allowed limited adjustments for age, four geographic regions, health status for groups new to coverage and a wellness discount, within a band of 50 percent above or below the community rate. New plans follow the 2014 rules.

Example: A bakery that kept its 2009 plan unchanged is still rated under the older structure; Priya’s new plan follows the age-and-tobacco rules.

Learning objectives

By the end, you can…

  • Define a small employer the Maryland way and count employees correctly.
  • Explain guaranteed availability, disclosure, enrollment eligibility and renewability in the small group market.
  • Apply Maryland’s small group rating rules.
