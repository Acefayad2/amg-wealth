01

Types of dental treatment

Elena, a dental hygienist, explains the categories to Theo. Diagnostic and preventive services are exams, x-rays, cleanings, fluoride and sealants. Restorative services repair teeth: fillings, crowns, inlays. Oral surgery covers extractions and other surgery of the mouth. Endodontics treats the tooth’s interior, chiefly root canals. Periodontics treats the gums and bone. Prosthodontics replaces missing teeth with bridges, dentures and implants. Orthodontics straightens teeth with braces, often limited to children and carrying its own lifetime maximum. Plans group these categories into tiers that determine how much the plan pays.

Diagnostic and preventive

Exams, x-rays, cleanings, sealants

Restorative, oral surgery, endodontics, periodontics

Fillings, crowns, extractions, root canals, gum care

Prosthodontics and orthodontics

Bridges, dentures, implants, braces

02

Indemnity dental plans

An indemnity dental plan lets the patient choose any dentist. A scheduled plan pays a fixed dollar amount per procedure, such as 90 dollars for a filling, and the patient pays the rest; schedules are easy to understand but lag behind rising fees. A nonscheduled plan pays a percentage of usual, customary and reasonable charges by benefit category: typically 100 percent of preventive, 80 percent of basic services such as fillings and extractions, and 50 percent of major services such as crowns and bridges. A combination plan uses a schedule for some services and percentages for others. Dental HMO and PPO networks apply the same categories with network pricing.

03

Deductibles, maximums and the arithmetic

Most dental plans carry a small annual deductible, often 50 dollars, waived for preventive care, and an annual maximum benefit, commonly 1,000 to 2,000 dollars per person, which resets each year. Marcus needs a crown with a UCR charge of 1,200 dollars under a 100 80 50 plan with a 50 dollar deductible. He pays the deductible, then 50 percent of the remaining 1,150, which is 575, for a total of 625; the plan pays 575. If the plan has a 1,500 dollar annual maximum and he already used 1,200 this year, the plan pays only 300 and he pays the rest. Maximums, not percentages, often decide the bill.

04

Exclusions, limitations and predetermination

Dental plans exclude cosmetic work such as whitening, replacement of lost or stolen dentures, experimental procedures and often teeth missing before coverage began, the missing tooth clause. Limitations include frequency limits, two cleanings a year, waiting periods of six to twelve months for major services, and the least expensive alternative treatment rule, which pays for the cheapest professionally acceptable fix. For costly work above a threshold, often 300 dollars, plans ask for predetermination of benefits: the dentist submits the treatment plan, the insurer states what it will pay before work begins, and the patient can decide with the numbers in hand. Predetermination is an estimate, not a guarantee.

05

Group dental design

Priya may add dental as part of the medical plan or as a separate plan. An integrated deductible counts dental and medical expenses toward one combined deductible, which can leave routine dental unpaid until the larger deductible is met. A stand-alone dental plan has its own small deductible and annual maximum and pays preventive care from the first visit, which is why most employers choose it. Pediatric dental is an essential health benefit, so children’s dental must be available with individual and small group medical coverage, either embedded or through a stand-alone dental plan on the Exchange.

Integrated deductible

One combined medical and dental deductible

Stand-alone plan

Own small deductible and annual maximum; preventive paid at once

06

Minimizing adverse selection in dental

Dental care is predictable, so people who need expensive work are the first to sign up for voluntary dental coverage. Plans defend themselves with the tools you have seen before. Waiting periods of six to twelve months for basic and major services stop someone from enrolling for a crown and leaving. Participation requirements keep healthy mouths in the pool. Late enrollees may be limited to preventive benefits for the first year. Annual maximums cap the plan’s exposure, orthodontics carries a lifetime maximum, and the missing tooth clause excludes work on teeth lost before coverage. Each tool trades a little coverage for an affordable premium.

Least expensive alternative treatment

When two professionally acceptable treatments exist, the plan pays its share of the less expensive one. The patient may choose the costlier option and pay the difference. Producers should explain this rule before a client is surprised by a crown benefit based on a filling.

Example: A plan pays 80 percent of an amalgam filling’s cost even when the patient chooses a tooth-colored composite; the patient pays the difference.

Orthodontic benefits

Orthodontics is often limited to dependent children under 19, paid at 50 percent, and subject to a separate lifetime maximum, commonly $1,000 to $2,000, that does not reset annually. Treatment in progress when coverage begins may be excluded or prorated.

Example: Marcus’s daughter’s $5,000 braces produce a $1,500 lifetime orthodontic benefit under the plan.
