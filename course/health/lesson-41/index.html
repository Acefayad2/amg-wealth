01

One Exchange, two markets

The Maryland Health Benefit Exchange is the public corporation created in 2011 that operates Maryland Health Connection. It has two divisions. The Individual Exchange sells qualified health plans to individuals and families and is the only place to receive premium tax credits and cost-sharing reductions; it also screens applicants for Medicaid and the Maryland Children’s Health Program through a single application. The SHOP Exchange, Maryland Health Connection for Small Business, serves small employers with up to 50 employees. Carriers may sell the same plans off the Exchange, but subsidies flow only through it. Rosa shops the Individual Exchange; Priya may use SHOP.

02

Open enrollment in Maryland

Individual coverage can be bought only during open enrollment or a special enrollment period, a rule that fights adverse selection now that coverage is guaranteed issue. Maryland sets a longer window than the federal marketplace: open enrollment runs from November 1 through January 15 each year, with coverage starting January 1 for enrollments by December 31 and February 1 for later ones. Medicaid and the children’s program enroll year-round. Maryland also runs Easy Enrollment: a checkbox on the state tax return lets the Comptroller share information with the Exchange, which then opens a special enrollment window for uninsured filers.

03

Special enrollment

Outside open enrollment, a qualifying life event opens a special enrollment period, generally 60 days: losing other coverage, including a job-based plan, COBRA or Maryland continuation running out, or turning 26; marriage, birth, adoption or placement; a permanent move into Maryland or to a new rating area; release from incarceration; gaining citizenship or lawful presence; or certain changes in income that affect subsidy eligibility. Theo’s client who leaves a job in May has 60 days to enroll with coverage starting the first of the following month. Someone who misses both windows waits for November.

04

Employer choice in SHOP

In SHOP, Priya decides how much choice her employees get. She may offer a single plan. She may pick a metal level and let employees choose any plan at that level from any participating carrier, the employee choice option, setting a reference plan to define her contribution. Or she may offer all plans from one carrier. Whatever she chooses, she sets a contribution percentage, employees enroll online, and SHOP sends her one monthly bill. SHOP also lets her offer stand-alone dental. Employers with fewer than 25 full-time equivalents and average wages under the federal threshold may claim the small business health care tax credit for two years.

05

Referrals and the no-wrong-door rule

The Exchange must screen every applicant for every program. An applicant who turns out to be eligible for Medicaid or the children’s program is referred and enrolled there; a Medicaid applicant whose income is too high is referred to the Exchange for a qualified health plan with subsidies. Carriers, the Exchange and the Department of Health must coordinate so families with mixed eligibility, parents on a marketplace plan and children on the children’s program, are handled in one process. A producer who learns that a client qualifies for Medicaid must say so, even though Medicaid pays no commission.

06

Who may help

Three kinds of people assist Exchange applicants. Authorized insurance producers hold a Maryland license, complete Exchange training and may earn carrier commissions. Navigators and certified application counselors work for grant-funded organizations, complete certification and may not accept commissions or steer applicants to particular carriers. Carriers’ own representatives may enroll people in their plans. All of them must give accurate information about every option, protect applicant data and never charge the consumer a fee for enrollment help. Theo, as an authorized producer, can enroll Rosa in a subsidized plan and Priya’s bakery in SHOP.

More detail

Maryland’s young adult subsidy

Maryland has offered an additional state premium subsidy for young adults buying through the Individual Exchange, layered on top of federal tax credits, to encourage enrollment by the age group most likely to go uninsured. Eligibility and amounts are set by the General Assembly and change; check the current program before quoting it.

Example: A 27-year-old barista buying a silver plan may see a lower premium than the federal credit alone would produce.

Off-Exchange plans

Carriers may sell the same qualified health plans directly, off the Exchange, but no premium tax credit or cost-sharing reduction is available for off-Exchange coverage. A producer should check subsidy eligibility before enrolling a client off-Exchange.

Example: Rosa, eligible for a credit, should enroll through Maryland Health Connection rather than directly with the carrier.
