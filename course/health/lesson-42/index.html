01

What Medicare is

Dennis turns 65 in March. Medicare is the federal health program for people 65 and older, people under 65 who have received Social Security disability benefits for 24 months, and people with end-stage renal disease or ALS. It is administered by the Centers for Medicare and Medicaid Services. Part A, hospital insurance, is financed by the payroll tax and is premium-free for people with 40 quarters of covered work. Part B, medical insurance, is financed by premiums and general revenue. Parts A and B together are Original Medicare. Part C is Medicare Advantage and Part D is drug coverage, covered in Lesson 43.

Who

65 and older; 24 months of disability benefits; ESRD or ALS

Part A

Hospital insurance; payroll tax; usually premium-free

Part B

Medical insurance; premiums and general revenue

02

Enrollment periods

Dennis’s initial enrollment period is seven months: the three months before his birthday month, the month itself and the three months after. If he already receives Social Security he is enrolled automatically; otherwise he applies. If he misses it, the general enrollment period runs January 1 to March 31 each year, with coverage starting the month after he signs up. If he keeps working with employer coverage from an employer of 20 or more, he may delay Part B without penalty and use an eight-month special enrollment period after the employment or coverage ends. Late Part B enrollment carries a permanent penalty of 10 percent for each full year of delay.

03

Part A: hospital insurance

Part A covers inpatient hospital care, skilled nursing facility care after a qualifying three-day inpatient stay, home health care, hospice and blood after the first three pints. Costs are organized by benefit period, which begins at admission and ends when the patient has been out of the hospital or skilled facility for 60 consecutive days. Each benefit period carries a deductible, 1,736 dollars in 2026. Days one through sixty are then covered in full; days 61 through 90 require a daily coinsurance; and 60 lifetime reserve days with a higher coinsurance can be used once. Skilled nursing care is covered in full for days one through twenty, with coinsurance for days 21 through 100 and nothing after day 100.

04

Part B: medical insurance

Part B covers physician services, outpatient hospital care, durable medical equipment, laboratory tests, ambulance, many preventive services without cost sharing, and a limited set of drugs administered in a medical setting. Everyone pays a monthly premium, 202.90 dollars in 2026 for most people, more at higher incomes. After an annual deductible, 283 dollars in 2026, Medicare pays 80 percent of the approved amount and the beneficiary pays 20 percent with no out-of-pocket maximum. Part B does not cover routine dental, vision or hearing aids, custodial care, cosmetic surgery or most care outside the United States. These gaps are why Medicare supplement and Advantage plans exist.

05

Claims terminology

When Dennis sees a doctor, the approved amount is what Medicare allows for the service. A provider who accepts assignment agrees to take the approved amount as full payment, bills Medicare directly and collects only the deductible and 20 percent from Dennis. A participating provider accepts assignment for all patients. A nonparticipating provider may refuse assignment and charge up to 15 percent above the approved amount, the limiting charge, which Dennis pays as an excess charge. Providers who opt out of Medicare entirely bill privately under contract. Dennis receives a Medicare Summary Notice showing what was billed, approved and paid, and an advance beneficiary notice warns him before a service Medicare may not cover.

06

Dennis at 65

Dennis’s plan: enroll in Part A during his seven-month window because it is free. Since his employer has 60 employees and his group plan is primary, he may delay Part B without penalty and enroll during the eight-month special enrollment period when he retires. When he does, his six-month Medicare supplement open enrollment begins. A hospital stay under Original Medicare costs him the Part A deductible; a surgeon’s bill costs him 20 percent of the approved amount plus any excess charge if the surgeon does not accept assignment. Those gaps, and the absence of any out-of-pocket maximum, drive the next three lessons.

More detail

Dollar amounts are indexed

The Part A deductible, Part B premium and deductible, and coinsurance amounts change every January. Learn the structure, deductible per benefit period, 80/20 with no cap, and verify the current figures from Medicare.gov before quoting them to a client or expecting them on an exam.

Example: The 2026 Part B premium of $202.90 replaced $185 in 2025; the exam tests the concept of a monthly premium, not the exact figure.

The three-day rule for skilled nursing

Part A covers skilled nursing facility care only after a medically necessary inpatient hospital stay of at least three consecutive days, not counting the discharge day, and only for skilled care, not custodial care. Time spent in the hospital under observation status does not count.

Example: Gloria’s two-night observation stay followed by rehabilitation in a nursing home leaves the facility bill uncovered by Part A.
