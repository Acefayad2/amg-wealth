01

Marketing to seniors

Selling to Dennis and Gloria draws extra scrutiny. Maryland’s Medicare supplement rules require advertising to be filed with the Insurance Commissioner before use and prohibit cold lead devices, which are mailings that hide their insurance purpose, high-pressure tactics, and any suggestion of government endorsement. Twisting, misrepresentation and the use of titles implying Medicare expertise beyond a producer license are banned. Producers must make reasonable efforts to determine that a policy is appropriate: it may not duplicate Medicare, an Advantage plan or another supplement, and it may not be sold to someone on Medicaid except in narrow cases. Excessive insurance is itself a violation.

Advertising

Filed with the Commissioner; no cold leads or implied endorsement

Appropriateness

No duplication; reasonable effort to assess need

Excessive insurance

Selling more than one supplement is prohibited

02

Disclosure, return and replacement

At application the producer delivers the federal buyer’s guide, Choosing a Medigap Policy, and an outline of coverage that summarizes benefits and premiums in a standard format. Maryland gives the applicant 30 days after delivery to return the policy for a full refund, and that right must appear prominently on the first page. If the sale replaces another supplement, a replacement notice is signed, the application asks about existing coverage, and the new insurer must credit the time already served under the old policy, waiving pre-existing condition, waiting, elimination and probationary periods for benefits the old policy covered.

Application

Buyer’s guide and outline of coverage delivered

Delivery

30-day right to return on the first page

Replacement

Notice signed; credit for time served

03

Minimum standards and prohibited provisions

A Maryland supplement must provide at least the federally required minimum benefits, and its deductible and coinsurance benefits must change automatically when Medicare changes, so Dennis never finds a gap opened by a January adjustment. A policy may not duplicate benefits Medicare already pays, may not use exclusions or limitations stricter than Medicare’s, and may not be offered at an unapproved introductory rate. It may not be canceled or nonrenewed except for nonpayment or material misrepresentation, and a spouse’s coverage may not end merely because the insured’s coverage ends. If the insured becomes eligible for Medicaid, benefits and premiums are suspended for up to 24 months at the insured’s request.

04

Pre-existing conditions and compensation

A Maryland supplement may exclude a pre-existing condition for no more than six months, and may define one no more strictly than a condition for which a physician gave advice or treatment in the six months before coverage. Time spent under qualifying prior health coverage can shorten that waiting period. When one supplement replaces another, the applicant gets credit for time already served for benefits the old policy covered. Compensation rules protect buyers from churning: a producer’s first-year commission may not exceed 200 percent of the renewal commission, renewal commissions must continue for a set number of years, and a replacement sale earns only the renewal rate, so there is no bonus for moving Gloria from one supplement to another.

6 months

Maximum pre-existing exclusion and look-back

200%

First-year commission cap relative to renewal

Replacement

Paid at renewal rate only

05

Guaranteed issue in Maryland

Beyond the federal six-month open enrollment at 65, Maryland adds protections. People under 65 who qualify for Medicare because of disability must be offered Plans A and D for six months after Part B enrollment, and the Plan A premium may not exceed the average paid by policyholders 65 and older. Since July 1, 2023, Maryland has a birthday rule: for 30 days after each birthday, an existing policyholder may switch to a policy with equal or lesser benefits from any insurer without underwriting, and insurers must give notice of that right 30 to 60 days before the birthday. Group supplements that end must offer individual conversion, and continuation applies when membership ends.

Under 65

Plans A and D for 6 months after Part B; Plan A premium capped

Birthday rule

30 days after each birthday; equal or lesser benefits; no underwriting

Group ends

Conversion or continuation offered

06

Medicare SELECT

Medicare SELECT is a Medigap policy with a network. For full benefits the insured must use the plan’s network hospitals and sometimes physicians, except in emergencies, in exchange for a lower premium. Benefits are otherwise the standard lettered plan. A SELECT policyholder has the right to switch to the insurer’s standard version of the same or a lesser plan without underwriting if she moves out of the network area or at any time during a stated window. Theo explains to Gloria that SELECT saves money only if her hospital is in the network, and that the Maryland rules in this lesson apply to SELECT as fully as to any supplement.

Hospitals, sometimes physicians

Lower premium

For using the network

Emergencies

More detail

Medigap and Medicaid together

Selling a Medicare supplement to someone receiving Medicaid is generally prohibited because Medicaid already pays Medicare’s cost sharing. The exception is a person whose Medicaid pays only the Part B premium. A policyholder who later qualifies for Medicaid may suspend the supplement for up to 24 months and reinstate it if Medicaid ends.

Example: Gloria qualifies for Medicaid after a long illness; she suspends her Plan G and reinstates it two years later when her income changes.

Maryland’s birthday rule in practice

The birthday window lets policyholders shop price every year without health questions as long as they move to equal or lesser benefits. A Plan G holder may move to another insurer’s Plan G or to Plan N, but not to a richer plan. Insurers must send notice before the birthday.

Example: Dennis’s Plan G premium jumps at 70; during the 30 days after his birthday he moves to a cheaper Plan G without underwriting.
