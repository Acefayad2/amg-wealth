01

Why a license

Theo has joined AMG and wants to start selling health plans. Maryland requires a producer license before a person sells, solicits or negotiates insurance. Selling means exchanging a contract for money; soliciting means urging someone to apply; negotiating means discussing the terms of a contract with a buyer on an insurer’s behalf. The license names the lines of authority the holder may handle, such as life, health, property or casualty; for this course, the health line is accident and health or sickness. Licensing exists to set qualifications, create accountability to the Insurance Administration and give consumers a regulator to turn to.

Sell, solicit, negotiate

Each requires a license

Lines of authority

Health, life, property, casualty and others

Purpose

Qualifications, accountability, consumer protection

02

Qualifying and applying

A resident applicant must be at least eighteen, have the character and trustworthiness the law requires, pass the Prometric examination for the line sought, file a complete and truthful application and pay the license fee, currently 54 dollars. Maryland repealed its mandatory prelicensing education requirement effective October 1, 2024, so a course is no longer required, though the exam and later continuing education remain. An applicant who fails must wait at least four days before retaking the exam. False statements on the application are themselves grounds for denial or later revocation, so the application is a legal document, not a formality.

Age 18; trustworthy

Threshold requirements

Examination

Prometric; 4-day wait after a failure

Application and fee

Complete and truthful; $54

No prelicensing course

Repealed October 1, 2024

03

Producers, advisers and nonresidents

An insurance producer sells, solicits or negotiates for an insurer. An insurance adviser is paid a fee to examine policies and give advice, and Maryland requires a separate adviser license for that work; a licensed producer acting as a producer for a client is exempt from the adviser rules, so Theo may explain the plans he sells without a second license. A nonresident producer holds a resident license in good standing in a home state and obtains a Maryland nonresident license through reciprocity, with the application and fee but usually no Maryland exam. The home-state license is not permission to work in Maryland by itself.

Producer

Sells, solicits, negotiates

Adviser

Paid for advice; separate license; producers exempt when acting as producers

Nonresident

Reciprocity based on a home-state license in good standing

04

Business entities

AMG itself is a business entity. An agency or other business that sells, solicits or negotiates must hold its own license and must designate a licensed producer as its principal contact with the Administration. That designated producer keeps the entity’s information current and responds to investigations. The entity’s license never covers an individual’s personal selling; every person who sells for AMG needs an individual license, and calling salespeople independent contractors does not change that. Trade names are filed through the prescribed process. Misconduct by the entity’s owners or designated producer can put the entity’s license at risk as well.

Business entity license

The agency’s own authority

Designated producer

Principal contact; keeps records current

Individual licenses

Still required for each person who sells

05

Exceptions

Some people may work around insurance without a license, but only within the exception’s conditions. A clerical or administrative employee who does not solicit and receives no commission is exempt; the moment she urges a customer to buy a particular plan, she is soliciting. An employer administering its own employee benefit plan without insurer compensation is exempt. Insurer officers and employees who do not sell for commission, and people who merely refer a customer to a licensed producer without discussing coverage, fall under exceptions tied to what they actually do. Read the conduct and the compensation, not the job title.

Exempt

Clerical work; employer administering its own plan; referral without discussing coverage

Not exempt

Recommending a plan; discussing terms; earning commission

06

Temporary licenses and the rule of thumb

Maryland may issue a temporary license without examination to specified relatives, representatives or employees of a producer who has died or become disabled, so the business can be preserved; the Commissioner must grant or refuse it in writing within 30 days, and it expires 15 months after it takes effect. It is not inherited authority, and the holder still needs any required appointment before acting. The rule of thumb for every question in this lesson: identify the act, sell, solicit or negotiate; identify the person; ask whether the person holds the license that act requires, or fits a narrow exception.

Temporary license

Relatives of a deceased or disabled producer; 15 months

Decision

Commissioner answers within 30 days

Rule of thumb

Act, person, license or exception

More detail

The application is a legal disclosure

The individual uniform application must be true, correct and complete to the applicant’s knowledge and belief. A business entity application identifies its designated producer and ownership. Intentional material falsehoods on either are grounds for discipline under §10–126.

Example: Theo omits a prior license revocation in another state; the omission alone supports denial or later revocation.

Read exceptions narrowly

Section 10–103 lists the conditions for clerical employees and other exceptions, and §10–202 exempts a licensed producer acting as a producer for a client from the adviser subtitle. Neither creates a general right to sell or to give paid advice without the matching license.

Example: AMG’s receptionist may schedule Rosa’s appointment but may not tell her which disability policy to choose.
