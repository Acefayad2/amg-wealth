01

Two clocks

Theo’s license is good for two years, expiring at the end of his birth month. Renewal needs the renewal application, the fees and continued qualification. Continuing education runs on an earlier clock: the hours must be finished before the renewal is filed. The Administration sends renewal notices to the address and email on file, so a producer who moved and never updated his record may miss the notice and the deadline. Theo marks two dates on his calendar, the education deadline and the expiration, and treats the first as the real one.

02

Continuing education

A regular Maryland resident producer must complete 24 hours of approved continuing education in each two-year renewal period, including at least three hours in ethics, with the hours satisfying the requirements of the lines held. The statute requires the education to be completed no later than 15 days before the license expires. An older Administration chart still says 30 days; the current statute controls. Special provisions reduce the hours for producers with long service who file the required affidavit, limited lines have exceptions, and a nonresident satisfies Maryland through compliance in the home state. This course is not approved continuing education.

03

If the license expires

An expired license is not a grace period. Theo may not sell, solicit or negotiate until reinstatement takes effect. For up to one year after expiration he may reinstate by filing the application, paying the renewal fee, the fraud prevention fee and a 100 dollar reinstatement fee, and proving the required education. If he applies within 60 days of expiration, the reinstatement is retroactive to the expiration date; after 60 days it is prospective only, leaving a gap during which any sales were unlicensed. After one year the license is gone and he must apply as a new applicant and retake the exam.

04

Changes the Administration must hear about

A licensee must report a change of legal name, trade name, email address or home or business address within 30 days. A business entity has a separate ten-day rule for changes to its designated producer’s required information. A producer who changes legal residence to another state has a 30-day certification duty, and a producer moving into Maryland must apply for resident authority within 90 days. Trade names are filed with the Administration before use; adopting an assumed name on a website does not file it. Updating the agency’s letterhead changes nothing in the licensing record.

05

Reporting prosecutions and other states’ actions

Two reports protect the public. If a producer is prosecuted for a crime, other than a vehicle-related misdemeanor, he must report it to the Commissioner within 30 days of the initial appearance in court, with the charging document, any court order and other relevant legal documents. Waiting for a conviction is too late. If another state or jurisdiction takes an administrative action against his license, he must report that within 30 days of the final disposition, with the order or consent order. Theo’s AMG manager cannot report for him; the duty is personal, and failing it is itself grounds for discipline.

06

Theo’s compliance calendar

Picture Theo’s year. In March he moves and updates his address within 30 days. In June he finishes his 24 hours, including ethics, well before the 15-day deadline. In August he files the renewal before his birth month ends. In October he is charged with a misdemeanor unrelated to driving and reports it within 30 days of his first court date, attaching the charging document. Each event has its own clock and its own trigger. A producer who keeps these dates keeps the license; a producer who assumes the agency will handle them risks losing it.

More detail

Special continuing education rules are not blanket exemptions

The statute provides reduced hours for producers with the required long service who file an affidavit, exceptions for certain limited lines, waivers for hardship and reciprocity for nonresidents. Reaching a particular age does not by itself remove the requirement.

Example: A producer licensed continuously since 1985 may qualify for reduced hours after filing the affidavit; a 70-year-old licensed in 2015 does not.

A timely renewal protects the license

A complete renewal application filed before expiration keeps the existing license in effect until the Commissioner makes a final decision on it. Letting the license lapse and then applying is a reinstatement, with different fees and the 60-day retroactivity rule.

Example: Theo files his renewal a week before expiration; even if processing runs past his birth month, he may keep working.
