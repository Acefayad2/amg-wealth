01

Grounds for discipline

Section 10-126 lists what can cost Theo his license. Making an intentional material misstatement on a license application. Obtaining or trying to obtain a license through fraud. Violating the Insurance Article or a Commissioner’s order. Misappropriating, converting or unlawfully withholding money belonging to an insurer, insured or applicant. Intentionally misrepresenting the terms of a policy. Forging an insurance document. Being convicted of a felony or a crime of moral turpitude. Demonstrating a lack of trustworthiness or competence. Failing to obey a lawful order or subpoena. Having a license disciplined in another state. Failing to report as required. Any of these supports action against the license.

Conduct

02

What the Commissioner may do

After notice and an opportunity for a hearing, the Commissioner may refuse to issue or renew a license, place the licensee on probation with conditions, suspend the license for a period, or revoke it. Each has a different effect: a suspended producer may return when the period ends; a revoked producer must reapply and may be refused. Instead of or in addition to suspension or revocation, the Commissioner may impose a penalty of 100 to 5,000 dollars for each violation and may order restitution to anyone who suffered a financial loss. The Commissioner may also issue a cease-and-desist order requiring a person to stop a specific unlawful practice, with further penalties for ignoring it.

License actions

Refuse, probation, suspend, revoke

Penalty

$100 to $5,000 per violation

Restitution

Repay financial injury

Cease and desist

Stop the practice or face more penalties

03

Notice and hearing

Due process shapes the procedure. Before most license sanctions, the Commissioner gives written notice of the proposed action and the grounds, and the licensee may demand a hearing. Under Section 2-210, a written hearing demand states the grounds for the request; the Commissioner must grant and hold the hearing within 30 consecutive days, unless the parties agree to postpone it, or refuse it, and a failure to act is treated as a refusal. At the hearing the producer may appear, present evidence and be represented. The Commissioner then issues a written order. Where the public faces immediate danger, the law permits summary action followed by a prompt hearing.

Notice

Proposed action and grounds

Hearing demand

In writing, stating grounds

Hearing

Order

Written decision

04

Judicial review

A producer who loses before the Commissioner is not finished. Under Section 2-215 a petition for judicial review in the circuit court is generally due within 30 days after the order is served, or after a refusal to hold a hearing. The court reviews the record for legal error and substantial evidence; it does not retry the case. Filing the petition does not automatically stay the Commissioner’s order, so a revoked producer stays revoked unless the court grants a stay. Theo’s practical lesson: respond to every notice, keep records that show what actually happened, and never ignore a lawful order or request for records while a matter is pending.

Record review

No automatic stay

05

Insurance fraud

Insurance fraud is knowingly presenting a false or misleading statement, claim or document to obtain a benefit, premium, commission or coverage. It includes padding a disability claim, backdating an application, pocketing a premium, or helping a client misstate health history. Maryland prosecutes insurance fraud criminally, with penalties that rise with the amount involved and can include prison, and the Commissioner can add administrative penalties and restitution. The Insurance Fraud Division, created under Section 2-401 within the Administration, investigates suspected fraud and refers cases for prosecution. Producers are frequent targets of fraud and frequent sources of the evidence that stops it.

Fraud

Knowingly false statement to obtain money or coverage

Criminal penalties

Scale with the amount; possible prison

Administrative

Penalties and restitution

Fraud Division

Investigates and refers for prosecution

06

The duty to report

Section 27-802 requires a person with good-faith cause to believe insurance fraud has occurred to report it in writing; an independent producer reports to the Fraud Division, and producers working for an insurer report through the insurer’s anti-fraud process. Good-faith reports are protected from civil liability; knowingly false accusations are not. When Theo suspects a client inflated a claim, he does not quietly withdraw and hope; he preserves the facts, follows the reporting process and cooperates with the Division. Concealing suspected fraud to protect a commission is itself a path to discipline under Section 10-126.

More detail

Suspension, revocation and reapplication

A suspension ends on its stated date, and the producer resumes without reapplying, provided renewal and education duties were met. A revocation ends the license entirely; the former licensee may apply again only after the period the Commissioner sets and must show rehabilitation. A refusal to renew has the same practical effect as revocation.

Example: A 90-day suspension for late reporting ends automatically; a revocation for misappropriating premiums requires a new application and a showing of fitness.

Business entities and their people

Misconduct by a business entity’s owners, officers or designated producer can be grounds for action against the entity’s license when the entity knew or should have known of the conduct and did not report or correct it.

Example: If AMG’s owner diverted premiums and the agency ignored it, the agency license, not just the owner’s, is at risk.
