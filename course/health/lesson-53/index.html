Explanations & examples

01

The Administration and the Commissioner

The Maryland Insurance Administration is the state agency that regulates insurance, headed by the Insurance Commissioner, who is appointed by the Governor. The Commissioner enforces the Insurance Article and the regulations adopted under it. The Administration licenses producers and insurers, examines insurers for solvency, reviews and approves policy forms and rates, investigates consumer complaints, holds hearings, issues orders and penalties and publishes regulations and bulletins. Theo will deal with the Administration as a licensee, as the person complaints about him are sent to, and as the regulator whose rulings decide what his insurers may sell.

Commissioner

Appointed by the Governor; enforces the Insurance Article

Licensing and solvency

Producers, insurers, examinations

Forms, rates, complaints

02

Unauthorized insurers

A producer may act only for insurers authorized to do business in Maryland. Soliciting, placing or collecting premium for an unauthorized insurer is prohibited, and the producer who does it can be personally liable for unpaid claims as if he were the insurer, in addition to license discipline and penalties. Checking authorization takes a minute on the Administration’s website. The exception is surplus lines, which applies to property and casualty risks placed through licensed surplus lines brokers when no admitted insurer will write them; it does not open a door for health insurance from unauthorized companies.

Prohibited

Acting for an insurer without a Maryland certificate of authority

Consequence

Personal liability for claims; discipline; penalties

Check first

Verify authorization before placing business

03

Records and unlicensed staff

Producers must keep records of their insurance transactions, including applications, correspondence, premium receipts and the appointment documentation from Lesson 51, and must produce them when the Commissioner asks; electronic records must be retrievable within ten working days. Appointment records are kept for five years after termination, and other rules set their own periods. Unlicensed employees may perform clerical and administrative work, collect information, schedule appointments and refer customers to a licensed producer, as long as they do not solicit, discuss coverage terms or receive commissions. The line is conduct, not title, and the licensed producer is responsible for supervising it.

Records

Kept and retrievable; appointments 5 years; 10 working days to produce

Unlicensed staff may

Clerical work; scheduling; referral without discussing coverage

Unlicensed staff may not

Solicit; explain terms; earn commission

04

Commissions and their sharing

Compensation for selling, soliciting or negotiating insurance may be paid only to a producer licensed for that line, and a producer may share a commission only with another producer who is licensed. Section 10-130 allows renewal or deferred commissions to be paid to someone who held the required license and appointment when the business was written, so a retired producer may keep receiving renewals. It also permits payments to people who do not sell, solicit or negotiate, such as a bonus to a receptionist, provided those payments do not amount to unlawful rebating or inducements. Theo cannot split his commission with Priya for enrolling her staff.

Sales compensation

Only to licensed producers

Sharing

Only with licensed producers

Renewals

May continue to former licensees

Non-sellers

Permitted if not a rebate or inducement

05

Fees and illegal compensation

A producer may not collect premiums or charges different from those the insurer has filed, with narrow exceptions. One exception permits a placement service fee of no more than 15 percent of the premium when the insurer pays no commission on the policy; on a 1,000 dollar premium that ceiling is 150 dollars. Another permits passing through the actual expense of a client’s credit card payment, with disclosure of accepted payment methods and costs. A producer licensed as an adviser may charge a fee for advice under that license. Any other charge added to a commissioned policy is illegal compensation, however small and however willing the client.

Rule

Collect only the filed premium

15%

Placement fee ceiling when no commission is paid

Actual cost

Credit card expense, with disclosure

Adviser fee

Only under an adviser license

06

Fiduciary capacity and trust accounts

Premiums Theo collects belong to the insurer, and return premiums belong to the client. He holds both in a fiduciary capacity. Maryland regulations require premium funds to be kept in a premium account separate from operating and personal accounts; mixing them is prohibited commingling. Premiums from several carriers may share one premium account unless a carrier objects. Withdrawals are limited to paying carriers, transferring commissions actually or on average earned, with a carrier letter for averages, and returning premiums; the balance may never fall below the premiums, return premiums and deposits owed. Using premium money for rent, even briefly, is misappropriation and a ground for revocation.

Premium account

Separate from operating and personal funds

Permitted withdrawals

Pay carriers; earned commissions; returns

Floor

Never below amounts owed

Misappropriation

Grounds for revocation

More detail

Voluntary deposits are not commingling

A producer may deposit its own funds into the premium account to cover shortfalls or bank charges, and may hold several carriers’ premiums together, without violating the commingling rule. What is prohibited is mixing premium funds into an operating or personal account or drawing the premium account below the amounts owed.

Example: AMG deposits $2,000 of its own money into the premium account to cover bank fees; that is permitted. Paying payroll from the premium account is not.

Fees need a legal basis

Section 27–216 lists the circumstances in which a charge beyond the filed premium is lawful. Client consent does not create a new exception, and labeling a charge a processing or convenience fee does not change its character.

Example: A $25 processing fee added to a commissioned health policy is illegal compensation even if every client agrees to it.

Learning objectives

By the end, you can…

  • Describe the Maryland Insurance Administration’s duties and powers.
  • Explain the prohibition on acting for an unauthorized insurer, record retention and what unlicensed people may do.
  • Apply the rules on commissions, fees, illegal compensation and fiduciary handling of premiums.
