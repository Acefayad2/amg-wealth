01

Children to 25, and to 26

Maryland’s Section 15-418 applies to certain plans that offer dependent coverage. It includes unmarried child dependents under 25, including the insured’s grandchild or a child eligible under the state’s separate dependent coverage law. They receive the same benefits at the same premium as other covered dependents. The federal rule is different. When a plan covers a parent’s children, they can generally stay enrolled until age 26, regardless of marital or student status. On the exam, check whether the question asks about a child or a grandchild. Maryland’s section excludes accident-only, disability and long-term care coverage.

02

Newborns, adopted children and guardianships

Section 15-401 makes a newborn covered from the moment of birth and an adopted child covered from the date of adoption, defined as the earlier of the judicial decree or the assumption of custody pending adoption; a minor placed under a court or testamentary guardianship of at least twelve months is covered from the appointment. Coverage includes care for congenital defects and birth abnormalities. If the policy charges a premium for the child, the insurer may require notice and payment within 31 days after the birth, adoption or appointment to continue coverage beyond those 31 days. Insurers must offer family coverage on request regardless of marital status and must disclose when it is not included.

03

Support orders and disabled dependents

Under Section 15-405, when a parent is ordered to provide health coverage for a child, the carrier must enroll the child regardless of enrollment periods, at the request of either parent, the child support agency or the Department of Health, and may not refuse because the child was born outside marriage, is not a tax dependent, lives elsewhere or receives Medicaid. Carriers act on a medical support notice within 20 business days. Separately, a dependent child who reaches the limiting age but cannot support himself because of an intellectual disability or physical handicap, and who depends on the insured, stays covered as long as the incapacity continues, with proof furnished when the age is reached and periodically thereafter.

04

Mandated offers

A mandated offer requires the insurer to make a benefit available; the policyholder chooses whether to buy it. Section 15-801 is the model: insurers and nonprofit health service plans issuing group policies on an expense-incurred basis must offer the policyholder the option of benefits for the care of people with Alzheimer’s disease, including nursing home and custodial care, and a similar option for elderly people with other diseases the Commissioner designates, subject to reasonable limits such as copayments and maximums. Priya may accept or decline the option for her bakery; the carrier may not fail to offer it. Continuation rights after death, divorce and job loss are also required features of group contracts.

Mandated offer

Insurer must make it available; buyer decides

05

Mandated benefits

A mandated benefit must be included in the policy. Maryland’s list is long; the exam favors these. Mental health and substance use disorder treatment at parity with physical illness, including partial hospitalization for at least 60 days, under Section 15-802. Off-label drug uses recognized in standard references, Section 15-804. In vitro fertilization for policies with pregnancy benefits, up to three attempts per live birth and a 100,000 dollar lifetime limit, with a religious organization exception, Section 15-810. Reconstructive breast surgery after mastectomy. Hearing aids for minors, 1,400 dollars per ear every 36 months, Section 15-838. Surgery for morbid obesity meeting NIH standards, Section 15-839. Tobacco cessation drugs and two 90-day courses of nicotine replacement a year, Section 15-841.

06

Other requirements

Several Maryland rules complete the picture. Carriers must pay or respond to claims within 30 days, with interest on late clean claims, under Section 15-1005. Members may grieve adverse decisions and complain to the Administration, which can order independent review. A certificate holder whose spouse dies may add the children who were on the spouse’s policy within six months without evidence of insurability. Mandated benefits and offers bind insurers, nonprofit health service plans and HMOs alike. The Maryland Health Care Commission evaluates the cost of mandates. When a question names a Maryland requirement, ask three things: who must comply, who is covered and what must be offered or paid.

More detail

Mandates and essential health benefits

Maryland’s benchmark plan for essential health benefits incorporates the state mandates that existed when the benchmark was chosen, so individual and small group plans carry them as essential benefits. Mandates added later may require the state to defray the added premium cost in subsidized plans, which is why the Health Care Commission reviews each proposal.

Example: Hearing aid coverage for minors is part of Maryland’s benchmark; a brand-new mandate would be evaluated for its cost before it bound Exchange plans.

Religious organization exception

Section 15–810 requires an insurer to exclude in vitro fertilization coverage from a policy issued to a religious organization when the coverage conflicts with the organization’s bona fide religious beliefs and the organization requests the exclusion. The exception belongs to the organization, not to the insurer.

Example: A church-run school asks its carrier to omit IVF coverage; the carrier must honor the request for that policy only.
