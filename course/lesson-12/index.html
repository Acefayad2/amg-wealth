Explanations & examples

01

Premium money has a purpose

Aisha receives a premium payment while the agency is short on cash. That money is not a convenient loan for rent. A producer handles money belonging to others with fiduciary responsibilities. Misappropriating, converting or unlawfully withholding money can support license discipline. Keep an accurate record of receipt, the client, the insurer, the purpose and the amount. The fact that a commission will eventually be earned does not make the entire premium the agency's income. Follow the authorized payment and accounting process from the start.

02

Prompt remittance or a premium account

Maryland's premium-account regulation defines prompt remittance as sending premium money to the carrier or insured no later than the close of the fifth business day following receipt. A producer who does not make prompt remittance must maintain the premium in one or more premium accounts, separate from operating and personal accounts. Do not count business days as calendar days. A premium account is restricted money, not an everyday spending account. A documented process should show where the money is and who is entitled to it.

03

Permitted deposits and withdrawals

Not every mixture of funds is the same. Maryland permits voluntary deposits into a premium account and generally allows multiple carriers' premiums there unless prohibited by a carrier or a special rule. It prohibits mixing the premium account with an operating or personal account. Withdrawals are limited to authorized purposes, such as paying carriers, transferring permitted commissions and making authorized returns. A withdrawal cannot leave less than the required aggregate net premiums, return premiums and unremitted deposits. Reconcile the account; do not guess the available balance from the bank total.

04

Who can receive sales compensation?

Compensation for selling, soliciting or negotiating insurance generally goes to a licensed producer, subject to statutory exceptions. Paying through a different label does not cure unlicensed selling. Maryland also permits certain renewal or deferred commissions to a person who formerly held the required life producer license and appointment. Other permitted payments or assignments to people who do not sell, solicit or negotiate must still avoid unlawful rebating or inducements. Identify what the person actually did, their required authority when applicable, and the kind of compensation.

05

A fee needs legal authority

A producer cannot simply add any charge the customer will tolerate. Maryland restricts collecting premiums or charges different from those applicable to the insurance, with specified exceptions. One exception permits a placement-service fee of no more than fifteen percent of the premium when the insurer does not pay commissions. For a one-thousand-dollar premium, that ceiling is one hundred fifty dollars. It is not permission to add fifteen percent to every commissioned policy. Verify the applicable exception, disclose accurately and keep a record of the authorized charge.

06

Records have different clocks

Record retention depends on the document and rule. Maryland appointment records are kept while the appointment is active and at least five years afterward. Under the annuity recommendation rule, covered transaction records are maintained for seven years after the insurer completes the transaction. An insurer may keep those records for a producer, but the producer should verify that arrangement rather than assume it exists. Preserve readable, retrievable documents and signatures. Good records connect the advice, disclosure, payment and final transaction so another reviewer can understand what actually happened.

Active appointment + at least 5 years

Annuity recommendation

7 years after completed transaction

Reliable file

Readable, complete and retrievable

More detail

A permitted voluntary deposit is not ordinary commingling

COMAR 31.03.03.02 permits voluntary deposits and specified withdrawals. Mixing a premium account with ordinary operating or personal accounts remains prohibited. Multiple carriers’ premiums can share an account unless a carrier or the substantial-interest rule prohibits it.

Example: An owner can make a permitted voluntary deposit to assure account adequacy; that does not turn the account into a source for personal purchases.

Commission and interest transfers have conditions

Permitted withdrawals include actual or average commissions. Using average commissions requires a carrier letter stating the percentage. Transferring bank interest to an operating account requires the carrier’s written consent. A withdrawal may not reduce the balance below the required aggregate obligations.

Example: A bank balance of $5,000 is not proof that all $5,000 can be withdrawn as agency income.

Compensation is tied to the real activity

§10–130 distinguishes sales compensation, permitted renewal or deferred commissions for former licensees, and certain payments or assignments to people who do not sell, solicit or negotiate. The latter still cannot violate the referenced anti-rebate and inducement provisions.

Example: Calling a payment a marketing fee does not authorize an unlicensed person to persuade clients about policy terms.

Record periods are not interchangeable

Appointment records must be retrievable within ten working days of the Commissioner’s request when stored electronically. The seven-year annuity rule covers the collected consumer information and other information used to support the recommendation. Replacement records and illustrations have their own specific rules, addressed in the replacement lesson.

Example: Five years after an appointment ends is a different starting event from seven years after an annuity transaction is completed.

Actual credit-card expenses and payment choices

§27–216(b)(4) permits collection of actual expenses incurred by a producer for paying a policy premium by credit card, with the required disclosure of accepted payment methods and those expenses. Such point-of-service expenses are not treated as premium under that paragraph. This does not justify an arbitrary undisclosed surcharge.

Example: The producer describes the actual allowed expense and available payment alternatives before the client chooses how to pay.

Learning objectives

By the end, you can…

  • Apply Maryland’s premium-account and prompt-remittance rules.
  • Distinguish permitted withdrawals from misuse of funds.
  • Recognize compensation limits and record-specific retention periods.
