Explanations & examples

01

A settlement transfers something valuable

Daniel owns a life policy that no longer fits his circumstances. A third party offers money now in exchange for ownership or death-benefit rights. A settlement is different from surrendering the policy to the insurer for its surrender value. It is also different from taking an accelerated benefit under the original contract. Maryland's viatical settlement definition generally involves value below the expected death benefit in exchange for specified policy rights. The statute is not limited to terminally or chronically ill insureds. Evaluate the actual transaction rather than relying only on its marketing name.

02

Know whose interests each person serves

The viator is the owner or certificate holder seeking or entering the settlement. A viatical settlement broker negotiates for the viator for compensation and must have the required life-producer authority. Maryland deems the broker to represent only the viator and to owe that person a fiduciary duty, regardless of how compensation is paid. The provider enters or effectuates the settlement contract. A later purchaser or financing entity may acquire an interest from the provider. These are different roles. A broker cannot secretly become the buyer of a policy covered by that broker's brokerage agreement.

03

Illness definitions have a specific purpose

In Maryland's settlement statute, terminally ill means an illness or sickness reasonably expected to result in death within twenty-four months or less. Chronically ill includes inability to perform at least two activities of daily living, substantial supervision needed because of severe cognitive impairment, or the stated similar disability. Activities of daily living include bathing, continence, dressing, eating, toileting and transferring. These are statutory definitions for this context, not permission for an agent to diagnose a person. They can affect specified transaction rules and must be supported through the appropriate process.

04

Disclose the tradeoffs before the decision

Before making an offer, the provider must complete the required signed disclosure process. Daniel should understand alternatives, possible taxes, creditor exposure, effects on government benefits and rights that may be lost. The broker gives the required service disclosure at application and discloses compensation at least seventy-two hours before all parties sign the contract. Offers and related responses must reach the viator within seventy-two hours after the broker receives them. These are different clocks. Medical and personal information also require the stated disclosure and consent process. A rushed signature does not replace informed consideration.

05

Use the correct payment and rescission clocks

Daniel receives the settlement proceeds. Maryland gives him fifteen calendar days after receipt to rescind, subject to returning the required proceeds, premiums and loan interest. If the insured dies during that period, the contract is deemed rescinded under the stated repayment conditions. The provider must send funds within three business days after receiving the specified acknowledgment of the policy transfer and beneficiary designation. A separate rule generally restricts a settlement within two years after policy issue, with listed exceptions supported by certification or independent evidence. Do not substitute another state's deadlines.

06

An honest file protects the owner

Someone offers to alter Daniel's medical records to raise the settlement price. Knowingly presenting false or misleading material information for an application, financing, transfer or settlement claim can be a fraudulent viatical settlement act. Correct an honest error through the proper process; never invent a diagnosis, conceal material facts or forge a signature. The broker should protect the viator's interests and disclose the required information. Before proceeding, compare the alternatives, confirm authority and registration, review the offered value and transferred rights, and make sure Daniel understands what his original beneficiaries may lose.

More detail

Maryland uses a broad settlement framework

§8–605(b) says a viator is not limited to an owner whose insured is terminally or chronically ill. §8–601 also includes certain policy-secured financing and agreements for later ownership or beneficiary transfers, while excluding specified provider-financing-entity transactions. A regular insurer policy loan and a loan secured by cash value are excluded from that specified financing inclusion.

Example: Calling an agreement a future transfer does not by itself remove it from the statutory definition.

Authority and registration are distinct requirements

A broker under §8–601(k) is an individual licensed as a life producer who negotiates for a viator for compensation, with the stated attorney, CPA and accredited-planner exception when retained by the viator and not paid by the provider. §8–603 requires a provider to register before acting; its broker provision specifies registration no later than thirty days after negotiating a contract. §8–610 also addresses acting without registration in accordance with the subtitle. Verify and complete the applicable registration process before undertaking business; do not treat the deadline as permission to ignore the other requirements.

Example: For an exam item naming the provider, the provider’s pre-activity registration rule is different from the express broker deadline in §8–603.

A broker’s fiduciary duty is owed to the viator

§8–605 requires following the viator’s instructions and acting in the viator’s best interest regardless of payment source. §8–605.1 prohibits a broker’s direct or specified indirect purchase of a policy subject to the broker’s brokerage contract.

Example: Receiving compensation connected with a provider does not switch the broker’s statutory client from the viator to the provider.

The two-year restriction has specific exceptions

Under §8–610, the exceptions include a qualifying conversion whose combined continuous prior and converted coverage meets the twenty-four-month conditions, independently evidenced terminal or chronic illness arising within the stated period, and independently evidenced disposition of closely held corporate ownership interests. Required documents go to the insurer with the verification request and the provider’s attestation.

Example: A newly issued policy is not automatically eligible for immediate sale merely because an owner finds a willing buyer.

The health-status contact limits depend on life expectancy

The required disclosure describes contact no more than once every three months if life expectancy is more than one year, or no more than once per month if it is one year or less. §8–606 also requires communicating an ownership or beneficiary change to the insured within twenty days after the change.

Example: A provider cannot treat the shorter life-expectancy contact interval as the routine interval for every insured.

Verify coverage and keep the deadlines separate

Under §8–610.1, the insurer generally has thirty days to answer the properly supported verification request, including the specified signed authorization and form; the response fee may not exceed $50. That is separate from the three-business-day proceeds clock, fifteen-calendar-day rescission period and the broker’s seventy-two-hour duties.

Example: A verification request does not itself establish that payment to the viator is already due three days later.

Rescission returns the required amounts

Ordinary rescission under §8–606 requires repayment of proceeds and any premiums and loan interest paid by the provider. Death during the period triggers deemed rescission subject to repayment of proceeds and the specified premiums, loans and loan interest. Other policy coverage, riders and conversion rights may also be affected by a sale and must be disclosed.

Example: The fifteen-day right does not allow the owner to keep both all settlement proceeds and all transferred policy rights.

Material deception is different from a corrected mistake

§27–403(6) addresses knowing or willful presentation of materially false or misleading information in the specified settlement activities. Separate regulatory violations can involve registration, disclosures, payment or rescission duties even without that same fraudulent statement.

Example: An intentionally falsified prognosis is a fraud example; a promptly corrected accidental spelling mistake is not automatically proof of intentional material deception.

Learning objectives

By the end, you can…

  • Distinguish a third-party settlement, surrender and an accelerated benefit.
  • Identify the viator, broker, provider and later purchaser.
  • Apply Maryland disclosure, timing and anti-fraud rules.
