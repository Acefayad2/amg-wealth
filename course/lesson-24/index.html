Explanations & examples

01

Different evidence answers different questions

The underwriter reviews Marcus's application and the producer's report. A recent treatment answer may lead to an attending physician statement, often called an A P S. That statement comes from the treating medical professional and can clarify history, diagnosis, treatment or follow-up. It is different from the producer's observations and different from a newly ordered examination. The insurer may use several sources together. A producer should arrange the authorized process, not ask a client to rewrite the doctor's record. If information conflicts, obtain clarification through the proper source instead of choosing whichever version produces a better price.

02

Medical evidence is handled through consent

An insurer may request an examination, laboratory information or other permitted evidence under its underwriting process. The requirements depend on the product, amount and applicant facts. No examination does not necessarily mean no underwriting. Accelerated processes can still evaluate authorized information. If an HIV test is required, follow the applicable informed-consent process and protect the result. An agent does not diagnose HIV from appearance, behavior or a laboratory number. Do not promise that a medical condition always produces a particular outcome. The insurer evaluates permitted evidence under its standards and the law, with the required authorizations and notices.

03

A consumer report is not a policy decision

An investigative consumer report can include information gathered through personal interviews about character, general reputation, personal characteristics or mode of living. It is distinct from a physician's statement. Consumer-report rules protect access, accuracy and privacy. The insurer must have a permissible purpose and follow the applicable notice and disclosure requirements. If report information contributes to a denial, higher rate or other adverse action, the required notice identifies the reporting agency, states that it did not make the decision, and explains dispute and free-report rights. Even a partial contribution can trigger the notice.

04

MIB information helps identify discrepancies

M I B is a consumer-reporting source used by participating insurers. Its information can alert an underwriter to facts that need clarification, including a discrepancy with an application. It is not a complete medical chart and does not itself issue or deny the policy. Obtain the required permission for medical report information and investigate the actual facts through authorized sources. Marcus can request his consumer file and dispute inaccurate information using the stated process. Do not assume every person has a file, or that an old report proves the same circumstances exist today. Accurate current evidence matters.

05

Classify the risk described by the facts

A preferred risk meets the insurer's more favorable criteria and may qualify for a lower rate. A standard risk fits the insurer's ordinary assumptions for that class. A substandard risk is an accepted higher risk and may receive a rated premium or other permitted terms. A declined application means the insurer does not accept that application under its current requirements. Substandard and declined are not the same result. Classification depends on the actual carrier and product. A producer should explain the offered terms accurately and avoid promising that another insurer must make the same decision.

06

Fair selection has legal limits

A higher premium is not automatically unfair discrimination when a lawful, supported risk difference explains it. But stereotypes and prohibited classifications are not underwriting evidence. Maryland protects applicants against specified unfair treatment and restricts use of domestic violence information. An insurer cannot use a person's status as an abuse victim to take an adverse action forbidden by the statute. The statute separately permits consideration of mental or physical medical conditions regardless of cause. It also does not require payment to someone who willfully caused the covered injury. Apply the actual facts and the legal rule. Fair underwriting combines relevant evidence, lawful standards and a clear explanation of the result.

More detail

Compare the information sources

An APS supplies information from the treating medical professional. A new examination or laboratory test supplies newly collected evidence. An investigative consumer report can use interviews about the applicant’s character or mode of living. A producer report supplies relevant field observations.

Example: A request to the treating physician for a treatment history is an APS request, not an investigative neighborhood interview.

The adverse-action notice has concrete rights

When a consumer report contributes to the adverse action, the notice gives the reporting agency’s contact details, explains that the agency did not decide the action, and explains the right to dispute inaccurate or incomplete information and request a free report within 60 days. Other applicable state requirements remain relevant.

Example: A report does not need to be the sole or primary reason to trigger the federal notice.

Not everyone has an MIB consumer file

MIB explains that its underwriting file depends on an application through a member carrier and information of underwriting significance within the stated period. The linked request process provides access and correction information.

Example: Do not infer a clean bill of health merely because no MIB file is found.

A rating is an offered classification

A substandard offer may use an additional premium, such as a table rating or an applicable flat extra, under the insurer’s terms. A decline is a different outcome. Neither result measures the applicant’s personal worth.

Example: If the company accepts coverage with a higher-risk premium, calling the application declined would misstate the offer.

Domestic-violence protection is specific

Insurance §27-504 restricts use of abuse information or victim status for the listed adverse actions, including refusing life coverage, adverse rating and specified claim or termination actions. Subsection (e) separately preserves consideration of mental or physical medical conditions regardless of cause. Other statutory restrictions still apply.

Example: Do not infer either that victim status may be used as a surcharge or that the law prevents consideration of every medical condition.

Learning objectives

By the end, you can…

  • Identify the source and purpose of underwriting information.
  • Distinguish preferred, standard, substandard and declined outcomes.
  • Apply privacy, consumer-report and Maryland domestic-violence protections.
