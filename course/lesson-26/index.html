Explanations & examples

01

Separate four questions about the policy

Marcus wants protection while his children are financially dependent. A term policy covers a specified period if the contract stays in force. Ordinary term generally has no cash accumulation. Ask four separate questions: how long protection lasts, how the death benefit changes, how long the premium is guaranteed, and what rights exist afterward. A level benefit does not by itself promise a level premium forever. Read the schedules. Maryland also requires clear language about whether a death on the term's expiry date is covered. A single word such as term cannot settle all those questions.

02

Level benefit and level premium are different

A stated twenty-year policy keeps a five hundred thousand dollar death benefit and a guaranteed annual premium of six hundred dollars during that period. The benefit and premium are both level under these particular terms. If the contract permits renewal afterward at a higher scheduled rate, that does not extend the original six hundred dollar guarantee. Annual renewable term uses one-year periods and commonly increases its renewal premium as the insured ages. A lower starting premium should be compared with future costs and the length of the need, not treated as the price for every future year.

03

Renewal continues term protection

A renewable provision gives the owner the stated right to continue term coverage without new evidence of insurability. The right still has conditions, such as age limits, premium payment and the available renewal period. It does not guarantee the old premium or permit renewal after every stated limit. Maryland requires the actual renewal premiums to appear in an individual renewable term policy. Its rule has a separate provision for wholesale and group coverage. For Marcus, worsening health makes an available renewal right valuable, but he must still consider the renewal price and exercise the right on time.

04

Conversion changes the kind of protection

A conversion provision allows an eligible owner to exchange term coverage for an available permanent policy without new evidence of insurability, within the stated limits. Conversion is different from renewing another term period. Marcus develops a medical condition but still has an available conversion right. He should check the deadline, eligible permanent products, allowed amount and premium basis. The new premium is commonly based on attained age, meaning his age at conversion, under the contract's rules. No new health evidence does not mean no new premium. Missing the conversion window can remove that particular right.

05

Match the benefit pattern to the need

A decreasing term design reduces its death benefit according to a schedule, often to address a declining obligation. Its premium does not necessarily decrease; many designs keep it level. In an increasing term design, the benefit grows under the stated formula or schedule. These labels concern the benefit, not a cash-value account. Suppose a teaching policy starts at one hundred thousand dollars and adds five thousand dollars at each anniversary. After two increases, its benefit is one hundred ten thousand dollars. Use the stated formula rather than assuming every increasing design compounds a percentage.

06

A premium refund depends on the contract

Return-of-premium term adds a contractual refund feature if the insured survives the stated period and the required conditions are met. It commonly costs more than otherwise comparable term without that feature. Do not promise a full refund after any early cancellation or assume every rider charge is included. In a simplified example, one thousand dollars of qualifying annual premiums paid for twenty years totals twenty thousand dollars. That arithmetic does not create a guaranteed investment return. Compare the protection, total cost, refund conditions and alternatives. The policy should fit the client's need and sustainable budget.

More detail

Maryland requires clear term-expiry language

COMAR 31.04.17.09 requires clear wording on whether a loss occurring on the term expiry date is covered. Follow the actual wording and time rather than assuming that every policy ends at the same instant.

Example: A policy ending before an anniversary and one covering death on that anniversary are different fact patterns.

Renewal and conversion may have different deadlines

The Maryland rule requires the brief renewable or convertible description to identify the exercise period unless the option applies throughout the policy’s term or renewal. A policy may allow renewal longer than conversion.

Example: A right to renew to a later age does not prove that conversion remains available until that same age.

Renewable does not mean an unchanged price

For individual renewable term, the actual renewal premiums must be shown. The Maryland rule separately allows the specified wholesale or group table change on an anniversary with 30 days’ notice. Do not apply that group exception to every individual contract.

Example: A twenty-year level-premium period can be followed by much higher scheduled renewal premiums without making the original twenty-year guarantee false.

Decreasing benefits can have level premiums

The benefit schedule and premium schedule are separate. A teaching design that starts at $200,000 and reduces the benefit by $10,000 after each anniversary has $150,000 after five decreases, even if the annual premium stays $300.

Example: Do not answer that the premium must drop whenever the benefit drops.

Increasing can mean a fixed addition or a percentage

Read the actual formula. Adding $5,000 annually to an original $100,000 is different from compounding the prior year’s amount by five percent. Both calculations require the stated number of increases.

Example: After two increases, the fixed-addition example is $110,000; a stated five-percent compound example would be $110,250.

Return-of-premium features are not all identical

The linked carrier page illustrates one product. The course does not assume its issue ages, availability, cancellation values or refund conditions apply to all policies. Use the actual contract to identify qualifying premiums, survival requirements and early-exit values.

Example: Twenty qualifying $1,000 annual payments total $20,000 in the stated example; a separate excluded rider charge should not be added to the refund.

Learning objectives

By the end, you can…

  • Compare level, decreasing and increasing death benefits.
  • Distinguish renewal from conversion without assuming a permanent price.
  • Explain a return-of-premium feature using its actual conditions.
