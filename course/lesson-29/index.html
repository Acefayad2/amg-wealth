Explanations & examples

01

Variable describes investment exposure

Marcus is comparing permanent policies and notices that several names include the word variable. In variable life, the owner can allocate policy value among the offered investment options in a separate account. Results depend on the selected investments as well as policy funding and charges. The owner bears the specified investment risk and can lose value. This differs from a conventional fixed design's stated crediting guarantees. A separate account is not the same as an indexed interest formula. Ask what is invested, what can change, and which benefit, if any, has a contractual minimum. Similar product names do not establish identical risk.

02

Variable whole life can retain scheduled premiums

A traditional scheduled-premium variable whole life design combines fixed required premiums with investment-dependent cash values. It commonly includes a stated minimum death benefit if the required premiums and other conditions are satisfied. That minimum does not guarantee the cash value or every illustrated increase in the death benefit. Read the actual policy. In a teaching contract, the minimum death benefit is two hundred thousand dollars while investment results can produce additional protection. A decline in investment value does not by itself rewrite that minimum, but loans, missed premiums, or other contract provisions still need separate consideration. Identify the precise promise.

03

Variable universal life adds funding flexibility

Variable universal life combines investment options with the flexible-premium structure of universal life. Marcus may adjust payments within its terms, but policy deductions continue. Poor returns and reduced funding can work together to deplete available value. A specific no-lapse feature may provide protection if its conditions are met; the product name alone does not supply that guarantee. In comparing variable whole life with variable universal life, separate two questions. Who bears investment risk? Both variable designs expose the owner to the specified investment results. How are required payments structured? Scheduled premiums and flexible funding describe different designs.

04

Show how loss and charges combine

Use a simplified account containing ten thousand dollars, all allocated to one investment option. Assume no additional premium and a ten percent investment decline before an assumed four-hundred-dollar deduction. The value first falls to nine thousand dollars and then to eight thousand six hundred. These are teaching assumptions, not a forecast or a real policy's calculation order. The example shows why investment performance and insurance charges must both be considered. It does not determine the death benefit, which follows a separate provision. A policy can have an important death-benefit guarantee while its variable account loses value.

05

Adjustable protection follows the contract’s choices

Elena's family needs change after a mortgage is repaid. An adjustable life contract can permit changes to the face amount, premium, payment period, or protection plan within its rules. Changing one element can change the others. For example, keeping a lower premium may require less protection or a different protection period under the stated plan. Increasing the insured amount may require evidence of insurability. An ordinary adjustment does not automatically replace the insured with a different person. Some contemporary products use adjustable in a universal-life name. Read the actual mechanics instead of assuming that the word adjustable means unrestricted changes.

06

Review the documents and use the right authority

Before a variable-life sale, explain the prospectus, investment options, expenses, surrender charges, and funding risks in language the applicant understands. Variable products involve securities requirements in addition to insurance authority. An insurance license alone does not grant every securities permission. Do not describe a variable policy as an ordinary bank savings account or promise a future return. For an existing adjustable policy, explain the requested change and document its acceptance through the insurer's process. Marcus and Elena should be able to state what is guaranteed, what can vary, what they must pay, and how their chosen protection could end.

More detail

FINRA requirements accompany variable-product authority

Variable life and variable annuities are securities products. A producer needs the applicable insurance authority and securities registration and must follow FINRA requirements. FINRA Rule 2211 requires accurate product identification and balanced descriptions of liquidity and guarantees; it does not allow an insurer guarantee to be presented as guaranteeing separate-account performance.

Example: A life license alone does not establish permission to sell variable insurance, and FINRA does not insure investment results.

A fixed required premium is not a fixed investment result

In the scheduled-premium variable whole-life example, payment timing is fixed while the separate-account values depend on performance and charges. The death-benefit minimum is a distinct contractual promise.

Example: A question that states fixed premiums and a conditional death-benefit minimum can still describe a variable product.

Variable universal life requires ongoing funding review

Separate-account performance, deductions, payments, loans and withdrawals can affect whether the policy remains funded. Any protection from lapse must be evaluated under its actual conditions.

Example: An owner cannot infer that flexible funding means premiums and charges can be ignored indefinitely.

Keep adjustable-life mechanics explicit

The outline’s adjustable-life concept includes changing the protection plan and premium schedule. Some modern carrier pages use adjustable as a synonym for universal life. The lesson therefore tests the mechanics stated in the question instead of relying on a marketing name alone.

Example: A lower payment with the same face amount may shorten the available protection under a stated adjustable plan; it does not automatically preserve every other term.

Use the stated order for a calculation

The narrated example applies the investment decline before the assumed charge: $10,000 × 90% − $400 = $8,600. Real policies specify timing, deductions and investment-unit accounting.

Example: If an exercise explicitly deducts the $400 first, the result after a 10% decline would instead be $8,640.

Learning objectives

By the end, you can…

  • Compare variable whole life and variable universal life.
  • Distinguish investment performance from a contractual death-benefit guarantee.
  • Explain how an adjustable contract can respond to changing needs.
