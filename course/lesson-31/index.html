Explanations & examples

01

A master policy covers eligible members

Elena enrolls in life coverage through her employer. The employer or qualifying trustees hold the master contract. Elena receives a certificate explaining her individual protection and rights. The group policyholder, insured employee, and beneficiary perform different roles. An employer group covers eligible employees or defined classes under the policy. A labor union can instead hold coverage for eligible members. Maryland's rules for these arrangements direct benefits to people other than the employer or union, as applicable. Do not confuse employee group protection with a business owning key person insurance for its own financial loss.

Master policyholder

Employer, union or another eligible holder

Insured member

Meets the group’s coverage conditions

Certificate

Describes individual protection and rights

02

Other groups have their own legal structure

A trust can hold group coverage for participating employers or unions under Maryland's rules. The trust or trustees become the policyholder. A qualifying association can also sponsor coverage, but merely gathering names to buy insurance does not satisfy the association requirements. A debtor group is different again: the creditor or another authorized holder insures eligible debtors in connection with their obligations. The payment addresses the insured debt, with any payable excess handled under the applicable rule. Identify the legal group and its purpose before applying an employer's eligibility or conversion provision to it.

Trust group

Qualifying employer or union arrangement

Association

Established organization; statutory conditions

Debtor group

Coverage connected to insured obligations

03

Group underwriting still manages selection

An insurer evaluates the group's characteristics, eligibility rules and participation. Stable membership and defined benefit classes can reduce the chance that only people expecting a near-term claim enroll. That is a selection issue, not a promise that no individual ever needs evidence of insurability. A late entrant or a person requesting an amount above a stated evidence-free limit may face additional requirements under the actual plan. Maryland also permits specified individual insurability exclusions or limits in these group statutes. Read the enrollment window and requested amount instead of answering every group question with no medical evidence.

04

Funding and benefits answer different questions

If Elena contributes toward her insurance premium, the plan is contributory. If the employer pays the full premium, it is noncontributory. Maryland's employer rule generally covers all eligible employees in that noncontributory arrangement, subject to written rejections and its stated insurability exception. Funding alone does not make the employer the death beneficiary. Benefits ordinarily follow the insured's designation and the policy's applicable payment provisions. Eligible dependents may have separate coverage, subject to the governing amount and eligibility limits. A spouse listed only as beneficiary is not thereby an insured spouse.

Noncontributory

Benefit recipient

05

Conversion protects a limited opportunity

Marcus leaves covered employment. Under the ordinary Maryland group conversion provision, an eligible person applies and pays the first premium within thirty-one days, subject to the notice-extension rules. New evidence of insurability is not required. The individual form, amount and premium follow the statute and available contract terms. Conversion is different from portability, which continues coverage under a specific group arrangement if that option exists. Ending the entire group policy also invokes a separate conversion provision. Revisit Part sixteen for those amount limits and notice rules. Do not assume the former group price continues forever.

06

Recognize the federal program names

Federal Employees' Group Life Insurance, or FEGLI, provides group term coverage for eligible federal employees, with Basic coverage and elected optional coverage. It does not build a cash value. Servicemembers' Group Life Insurance, or SGLI, provides term coverage to eligible service members. Veterans' Group Life Insurance, or VGLI, can continue term protection after qualifying military service when its enrollment and premium requirements are met. These are different programs with their own eligibility, deadlines and benefit schedules. Use the current government guidance for an actual enrollment. Do not replace a federal program's rules with a generic Maryland employer conversion deadline.

More detail

Certificates and master contracts have different roles

A certificate describes the insured member’s protection; the group policyholder holds the master contract. Individual life ordinarily uses an individual policy, while group life connects coverage to an eligible group and its terms.

Example: An employee with a certificate does not thereby own the employer’s master contract.

Recognize the qualifying group

Maryland separately authorizes employer, labor-union, trust, association and debtor arrangements. Under §17-203, qualifying trusts can involve multiple employers, unions, or their permitted combinations. Under §17-204, an association must initially have at least 100 people, two years of active existence, a genuine purpose other than obtaining insurance, and the specified governance, meeting and dues/contribution conditions.

Example: A newly created club of ten applicants formed solely to purchase coverage does not meet that association provision.

Benefit payment includes specific exceptions

The ordinary group provision in §17-307 pays the insured’s designated beneficiary, subject to the policy’s survival and permitted expense-payment provisions. A reserved certificate-disclosed right can allow up to $2,500 to an equitably entitled person for funeral or last-illness expenses. Creditor policies have a different scope under §17-301 and their debt-related rules.

Example: Do not assume the master policyholder receives every group death benefit.

Dependent coverage is separately defined

Under the covered Maryland arrangements in §17-209, dependent protection may include a spouse, domestic partner or eligible dependent child. The dependent amount cannot exceed the employee/member amount. The statute’s child definition is not a universal age-26 rule. See Part 16 for the statutory details.

Example: Naming a spouse as the employee’s beneficiary does not enroll that spouse for a separate death benefit.

Contributions do not establish one universal enrollment percentage

Employee premium participation makes a plan contributory. For the Maryland employer noncontributory rule, apply coverage of eligible employees with the written-rejection and insurability exceptions. Do not import a generic 75% contributory rule when the question has not supplied it and the applicable statute does not impose it.

Example: A question explicitly giving a plan participation requirement should be answered using that stated requirement.

Different conversion events have different rules

Ordinary employment or eligible membership termination, termination of the group policy/class, late conversion notice and death during the conversion opportunity are separate events. Part 16 teaches §§17-308 through 17-311. Portability is an available continuation feature when offered; it is not a synonym for every statutory individual conversion.

Example: A group policy terminating after six years and a person leaving a continuing employer plan are different fact patterns.

Use current federal eligibility guidance

FEGLI is group term with Basic and optional coverage. SGLI serves eligible service members. VA’s current VGLI guidance generally requires application within one year and 120 days after separation; applying within 240 days avoids the health-evidence requirement. These time periods have different purposes and remain subject to the program’s eligibility provisions.

Example: Do not call the 240-day no-health-review period the final application deadline.

Learning objectives

By the end, you can…

  • Distinguish the master policyholder from insured members and beneficiaries.
  • Explain why group coverage can still require individual evidence.
  • Recognize conversion rights and major federal life programs.
