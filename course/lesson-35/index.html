Explanations & examples

01

Reinstatement restores an eligible lapsed policy

Marcus let an ordinary individual life policy lapse after its premium grace period. Reinstatement may restore that policy when the required conditions are satisfied. Maryland's standard provision allows an application within three years from the due date of the first premium in default. It requires a written application, satisfactory evidence of insurability, overdue premiums, and payment or reinstatement of other policy debt with the allowed interest. It does not apply when the policy was surrendered for cash, its cash value was exhausted by debt, or paid-up term coverage has expired. Check eligibility before promising that a late payment alone restores insurance.

02

A reinstatement application has its own accuracy issue

Suppose Marcus applies within the permitted period but gives an inaccurate answer about a health change material to reinstatement. Maryland permits a contest of a reinstated life policy for fraud or misrepresentation material to that reinstatement, under the same period, conditions and exceptions that apply after original issue. The period is measured after reinstatement. This is different from treating every old statement as newly contestable without examining what the statute permits. Industrial life also has a different standard reinstatement window of two years. Keep the policy category, first default date, reinstatement date and actual disputed statement on a clear timeline.

03

Correct the benefit using the applicable rate

An age correction ordinarily adjusts the benefit to what the premium would have purchased at the correct age under Maryland's standard provision. Assume a teaching policy states one hundred thousand dollars, with two hundred dollars paid for the relevant premium period. The correct-age premium for that same stated coverage would have been two hundred fifty dollars. Under the stated proportional formula, two hundred divided by two hundred fifty equals eighty percent. The adjusted benefit is eighty thousand dollars. Actual policy formulas and statutory age-limit exceptions matter. Where a contract also adjusts for misstated sex, use its lawful rate and adjustment provision.

04

A suicide exclusion has a defined period

A suicide exclusion is separate from an incontestability provision. Maryland permits the stated individual-life exclusion for suicide, whether sane or insane, within two years from issue, subject to its special continuity rules when the same insurer replaces earlier coverage. Do not assume every new policy automatically restarts the full period for all previously insured benefits. The contract and statute also determine the amount payable when an allowed exclusion applies. Maryland sets a minimum tied to the policy's statutory reserve and specified adjustments. A more favorable policy may promise a premium refund. Read that actual promise instead of calling every excluded claim a zero payment.

05

War and other restrictions must be permitted

Maryland allows particular war or military-service restrictions and other specified exclusions. Read whether a policy uses a status-based restriction or one tied to the cause of death. A statement about military membership is different from a statement about death caused by war. The statute also addresses aviation, specified hazardous work or recreation, and certain residence conditions, with different limits. It prohibits excluding death from terrorism when the insured was a victim who did not participate. A producer must explain the actual policy restriction accurately. A general claim that every life policy excludes every military death would be wrong.

06

Do not falsify an application date

Maryland prohibits knowingly issuing premium-saving life insurance more than six months before the actual application date under the statutory conditions. It separately prohibits a producer from falsely dating the application earlier to obtain that lower age-based premium. Limited permitted policy backdating does not authorize changing when the client actually applied. Other prohibited provisions also matter. For example, a policy cannot reduce its benefit merely because the same insurer issued another policy on that life. Some medical-history or prior-rejection provisions have specific statutory limits rather than unrestricted cancellation power. Record the true facts, read the lawful contract, and identify the exact rule involved.

More detail

Reinstatement includes financial and underwriting conditions

Under §16-210, the standard ordinary-life window is three years from the due date of the first defaulted premium; industrial life has two years. The application must be written and accompanied by satisfactory evidence of good insurability. Pay all overdue premiums and pay or reinstate other policy debt. Section 16-210 states a 6% effective annual interest limit, compounded annually, for its reinstatement provision. Policy-loan interest must also be checked under §16-208: for policies issued on or after July 1, 1983, that section expressly includes reinstated policy loans and permits a fixed maximum of no more than 8% or the statutory adjustable maximum. Do not apply the 6% figure as a universal cap on reinstated policy-loan interest. Cash surrender, exhaustion of cash value by debt and expiration of paid-up term defeat the stated eligibility.

Example: An owner within three years still must satisfy the remaining conditions; the deadline alone is not approval.

A restored policy is not a blank check to contest everything

Section 16-107 concerns fraud or misrepresentation of facts material to reinstatement. It applies the original period, conditions and exceptions measured from reinstatement. It also addresses permitted exclusions and restrictions at reinstatement. Exchanges, alterations and conversions have additional rules in §16-106.

Example: Identify which application or change contains the disputed material statement.

The age rule has a precise exception

Section 16-205 generally adjusts benefits to the correct age. If the policy expressly limits insurable ages and the actual issue age was outside that limit, its special voidability rule applies during the insured’s lifetime within three years after issue and within thirty days after correct age is established, with its stated refund calculation. After three years, the policy cannot be voided on that ground; apply the benefit adjustment or specified actuarial determination.

Example: Do not turn the usual adjustment rule into either automatic cancellation or an absolute statement that cancellation can never occur.

Age and sex clauses are not identical in every product

The outline groups misstated age and gender. Maryland §16-205 expressly addresses age. A current Nassau filed prospectus supplies a product example adjusting a death benefit using the latest monthly insurance charge and the correct age or sex. That is an illustration of a contract mechanism, not a claim that every insurer uses that formula or that this product is currently sold.

Example: For a simplified proportional question, $300 paid against a correct $250 rate gives 1.20 times the stated amount when that formula is expressly supplied.

Maryland’s suicide continuity and payment provisions matter

Section 16-215(e) generally credits the issue date of prior coverage issued by the same insurer for the covered amount when its continuity conditions are met. Exceptions include prior coverage ending before the new issue or more than twelve months afterward; excess new benefits begin with the new issue date, and multiple prior policies have amount-specific rules. Section 16-215(b)(3)–(4) sets the permitted-exclusion payment floor using the statutory reserve with debt, dividend and other stated adjustments.

Example: Do not teach a universal restart or a universal refund-of-every-premium formula.

Restrictions have different scopes and time limits

Section 16-215 permits specified war/military and aviation restrictions. Its hazardous occupation or avocation and outside-continental-U.S./Canada conditions have two-year limits. The section excludes reinsurance, group life, disability benefits and additional accidental-death benefits from its stated scope. The actual policy can be more favorable and cannot exclude death of a nonparticipating terrorism victim.

Example: A status clause and a result-of-war clause can answer a military-death scenario differently.

Backdating a policy is different from falsifying the application

Section 16-104(a) prohibits knowingly issuing the described lower-premium policy more than six months before the application was actually made. Subsection (b) separately prohibits the false earlier application date used for the stated premium reduction. The section does not invalidate the contract or prohibit the exchanges, alterations and conversions it identifies.

Example: A producer cannot move the application date to make an impermissible policy date appear to be within six months.

Prohibited provisions require the statutory facts

Section 16-216 prohibits denial or reduction merely because the same insurer issued other insurance. It also restricts voiding for prior disease or treatment, with its specified recent-treatment and seriousness/materiality exception, and permits the stated prior-rejection provision only when the insurer shows it would have refused coverage had it known.

Example: A past doctor visit is not an unrestricted contractual permission to cancel a policy.

Learning objectives

By the end, you can…

  • Apply the standard Maryland reinstatement conditions.
  • Distinguish benefit adjustment from automatic cancellation.
  • Recognize the limits on exclusions and premium-saving backdating.
