Explanations & examples

01

Participation is eligibility, not a promised payment

Elena owns a participating whole life policy. Participating means it can share in the insurer's divisible surplus through policy dividends under its terms. A nonparticipating policy does not provide that participation. Neither label tells us whether the coverage is temporary or permanent. A declared dividend can reflect the insurer's experience with claims, investments and expenses. It is not a guaranteed future amount merely because dividends have been paid before. Keep the policy's contractual guarantees separate from a current dividend illustration. Under Maryland's rule, the insurer determines and apportions the stated surplus annually for participating policies. That duty does not guarantee a positive dividend every year.

02

Cash and premium reduction solve different needs

Assume Elena's insurer has declared a four hundred dollar dividend. Under the cash option, she receives that available dividend as money. Under premium reduction, the dividend is applied toward the premium due. If that premium is twelve hundred dollars, applying four hundred leaves eight hundred dollars to pay from other funds. The dividend has not erased every later premium. It is being used for this payment. Taking cash also does not mean surrendering the entire policy. Follow the selected transaction carefully: distributing a dividend, paying a premium and surrendering protection are different actions with different consequences for the contract.

03

Accumulation leaves the dividend to earn interest

Elena can instead leave the declared dividend with the insurer to accumulate interest under the policy's available option. Assume four hundred dollars remains for a full year at a hypothetical three percent simple annual rate, with no additions or withdrawals. Twelve dollars of interest brings the total to four hundred twelve dollars. The interest-crediting terms and access rights come from the actual arrangement. The declared dividend and the interest earned afterward are separate items. Interest on accumulated dividends generally creates taxable interest income, covered more fully in the tax lessons. Do not label the accumulation balance an extra death benefit purchased with insurance.

04

One-year term and paid-up additions buy different coverage

Under an available one-year term dividend option, the dividend buys additional temporary protection for the specified year under the contract's conditions. A paid-up-additions option instead buys an additional amount of permanent insurance that requires no more premiums for that purchased addition. Suppose a stated offer uses Elena's four hundred dollar dividend to buy two thousand dollars of paid-up additional whole life. The two thousand is added insurance, not a four hundred dollar cash account and not proof that her entire base policy is paid up. The amount a dividend buys depends on the applicable purchase basis. Check availability, eligibility and the actual quotation.

05

Earlier paid-up status requires sufficient actual values

Another outline concept is using dividends toward paid-up insurance or shortening the premium-paying period. Under an available arrangement, accumulated dividend values can help fund the amount needed to make the existing policy paid up sooner. That goal is different from buying a small paid-up addition each year. It is also different from choosing reduced paid-up nonforfeiture and accepting a smaller base benefit. Most importantly, a projection that future dividends will cover premiums is not confirmation that the policy is already paid up. If dividends fall, an owner relying on premium offset may need to resume payments from other money. Confirm actual contractual status with the insurer.

06

Read the available choices and default election

Maryland's participating-policy provision includes the stated cash, premium-payment, paid-up-addition and interest-accumulation choices, with a term-policy exception for the latter two. An insurer may offer additional choices. This does not make every textbook option mandatory on every policy. An ordinary participating policy must specify what happens if a different option is not elected within the stated thirty-day period after the dividend becomes payable. Elena therefore checks the actual option and deadline before deciding. On an exam, first identify whether the question asks about receiving cash, reducing a premium, earning interest, adding temporary coverage, adding permanent coverage or making the existing insurance paid up.

More detail

A dividend scale is not the owner’s personal return

Participation supplies eligibility under the policy. The insurer’s scale reflects its experience and is not a guaranteed investment yield on every dollar of premium or cash value. Nonparticipating and participating are separate classifications from term and permanent.

Example: A current illustration showing $400 next year does not make that dividend a contractual guarantee.

Read Maryland’s actual list and timing

Section 16-206 provides annual surplus determination and apportionment for participating policies and the specified credit timing: no later than the end of the third policy year for ordinary life and fifth for industrial life. Dividend payment cannot depend on paying a premium due on or after its payment date. Cash, premium then due, paid-up additions and accumulation are listed, with the stated term exception. Additional options may be offered.

Example: For ordinary participating policies, the specified default applies unless another available choice is elected in writing within thirty days after the dividend is payable.

A dividend can buy more insurance than its dollar amount

The dividend is the purchase payment; the amount of insurance purchased follows the applicable actuarial basis. A hypothetical $400 dividend might buy the offered $2,000 addition. That is not a 400% cash investment gain available for immediate withdrawal.

Example: A paid-up addition has its own insurance and cash-value effects; the base policy can still require premiums.

One-year term is an available-contract feature

The cited SEC-filed policy demonstrates a one-year term dividend option, including its own eligibility and election conditions. It is a contract example, not proof that every Maryland participating policy must offer the feature or that no evidence can ever be required.

Example: Temporary additional insurance and permanent paid-up additions have different durations.

Paid up is stronger than a projection of no out-of-pocket payments

The paid-up-insurance objective uses available dividend values under an actual arrangement to shorten or complete the existing premium obligation. A projected premium offset may still depend on future nonguaranteed dividends and use of policy values. Confirm the insurer’s actual calculation and paid-up status; do not promise a guaranteed stop-payment year from a dividend projection.

Example: A paid-up addition, reduced paid-up nonforfeiture and a fully paid-up original policy are three different descriptions.

Learning objectives

By the end, you can…

  • Distinguish participating eligibility from guaranteed dividends.
  • Apply all six dividend-option concepts in the exam outline.
  • Explain why a projected premium offset is not confirmed paid-up insurance.
