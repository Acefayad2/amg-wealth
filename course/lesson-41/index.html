Explanations & examples

01

An acceleration pays part of a life benefit earlier

In this scenario, Marcus is seriously ill and asks whether his life policy can provide money while he is living. An accelerated benefit pays an eligible amount early under the policy's qualifying conditions and reduces the death benefit otherwise payable. It is not automatically an extra benefit on top of the full original death protection. It also differs from borrowing against cash value or selling a policy to an outside settlement provider. Identify the actual feature first. Maryland's accelerated-benefit chapter requires terminal illness among its qualifying events for products within that chapter. It does not mean every illness automatically qualifies for payment.

02

Read the medical trigger instead of guessing

A contract may describe a limited life expectancy, a condition requiring extraordinary intervention, or expected permanent confinement in an eligible institution. These are specific triggers, not interchangeable labels for any hospital visit. A short rehabilitation stay is not automatically confinement expected to last for the rest of the insured's life. Some approved designs include chronic-illness conditions involving daily activities or severe cognitive impairment. Read the required certification, degree of assistance, permanence and other conditions. A family member's description of being very sick is important context, but it does not replace the medical evidence required by the actual benefit provision.

03

Daily activities are specific personal-care functions

Activities of daily living include bathing, dressing, eating, toileting and transferring. Continence, meaning control of bladder and bowel function, is also included. Transferring means moving between positions such as a bed and a chair. Shopping, driving and managing investments are not substitutes for those named activities. Suppose a fictional rider requires permanent inability to perform two specified activities without substantial assistance. A physician's evidence of qualifying help with bathing and dressing addresses two named activities, but the remaining rider conditions still must be met. Do not turn that example into a universal two-activity rule for every acceleration product. A separate cognitive-impairment trigger can require substantial supervision under the actual wording.

04

The reduction can exceed the cash received

Assume a two hundred thousand dollar death benefit and an approved acceleration of one hundred thousand dollars of that benefit. In this simplified example, a stated ten thousand dollar discount reduces the immediate cash payment to ninety thousand dollars. The remaining death benefit is one hundred thousand dollars, because one hundred thousand of the original benefit was accelerated. It is not one hundred ten thousand merely because the owner received ninety thousand cash. Real contracts can use different charges, discounts or lien methods. Read the insurer's before-and-after statement and any outstanding debt. Never infer remaining protection by subtracting only the check from the old face amount.

05

Cash value, debt and premiums need their own review

The death-benefit change is only one part of the calculation. Acceleration can affect cash value, accumulation accounts, outstanding loans, future loan access and premium obligations. Maryland's actuarial rule addresses proportional cash-value reduction. An alternative approach places a lien against the policy, with permitted interest that can reduce value available later. Do not promise that all existing policy debt disappears or that every premium stops. At the time the benefit is claimed, the insurer must explain continuing premium requirements needed to keep the policy in force. Marcus needs those continuing obligations in writing so he can understand what coverage and resources remain after the payment.

06

Disclosures come before an informed decision

For an agent-solicited application under Maryland's rule, the written benefit description and effects disclosure is provided before or with the application and acknowledged by the applicant and writing agent. Additional information is required when acceleration is requested. Tax treatment requires its own analysis: qualifying terminal-illness benefits can be excluded from federal income, while chronic-illness exclusions have additional rules. Do not promise every payment is tax-free or that government-benefit eligibility is unaffected. The insurer must provide the required tax and benefit-eligibility disclosures. The person holding an assignment, or an irrevocable beneficiary, may also need to provide the required concurrence. Review the trigger, calculation and continuing obligations together before treating the transaction as understood.

More detail

Keep acceleration and long-term care product rules distinct

COMAR 31.09.16 applies to the stated individual and group life acceleration provisions except those subject to Insurance Article Title 18. Products offered under this chapter may not be marketed as long-term care insurance or as providing long-term care benefits. A chronic-illness feature does not erase that distinction.

Example: This lesson teaches life-benefit acceleration, not a separate health or long-term care licensing course.

Medical triggers are defined in the contract

The chapter includes terminal illness and permits the stated life-threatening, extraordinary-treatment, permanent-confinement and chronic-illness events. Its chronic-illness definition addresses permanent functional inability or permanent severe cognitive impairment with substantial supervision.

Example: A temporary difficulty after a routine procedure does not necessarily meet a permanent-condition trigger.

Contract eligibility and federal tax eligibility are different questions

IRS Publication 525 describes the terminally ill exclusion using physician certification that death can reasonably be expected within 24 months. A rider can use a different contractual benefit trigger. Chronic-illness exclusions have qualified-service and payment-method rules, and certain business-related recipients are excepted. Do not import the federal 24-month definition as the trigger in every issued rider.

Example: No annual per-diem dollar limit is frozen into this lesson; use the relevant tax-year IRS guidance.

Maryland identifies disclosure timing and recipients

COMAR 31.09.16.07 governs application descriptions, signed acknowledgments, tax warnings, applicable numerical illustrations and disclosure of charges. A requested-acceleration statement goes to the owner or certificate holder and irrevocable beneficiary. It explains changed values, possible tax and possible effects on Medicaid or other government benefits. Revised disclosures and an amended schedule or group notice follow where required.

Example: Agent-solicited application disclosure is due before or concurrently with application, not merely after a claim is approved.

Concurrence and effectiveness have separate provisions

Under .05, obtain the required signed concurrence from an assignee or irrevocable beneficiary before payment; the insurer-as-assignee exception applies. Under .08, accident coverage is effective on the policy or rider effective date, and illness coverage no more than thirty days later. This is not a universal thirty-day delay in paying a submitted claim.

Example: A commencement rule and a claim-processing requirement are different matters.

Compare all values under the actual financing method

Under .11, the permitted methods include a charge, actuarially discounted payment or interest-bearing lien. A proportional cash-value reduction and a lien method have different mechanics. The rule restricts cash-value and loan treatment; a proportional reduction cannot apply more than a proportional part of existing loans to repayment.

Example: A quote showing $100,000 accelerated and $90,000 paid does not necessarily leave $110,000 of an original $200,000 benefit.

Acceleration does not automatically waive premiums

COMAR .09 permits a waiver feature but requires explanation of any continuing premiums at claim time. Whether future premiums change depends on the issued benefit and financing method.

Example: An owner receiving a living payment may still need to pay premiums to maintain remaining coverage.

Learning objectives

By the end, you can…

  • Recognize the stated illness, confinement and daily-living triggers.
  • Distinguish the amount accelerated from the cash actually received.
  • Apply Maryland disclosures and identify effects on values, debt, premiums and tax.
