Explanations & examples

01

A single premium does not always mean immediate income

Rosa has a lump sum available and wants retirement payments to begin next month. A single-premium immediate annuity uses one contribution to establish income beginning within one year of purchase. Now change the facts: David places a lump sum into a contract but plans income for ten years later. That is deferred income, even though he paid only once. Immediate and deferred describe income timing. Single and periodic describe how money goes into the contract. Keep those classifications separate. Also distinguish the beginning of payments from their duration. An immediate annuity can provide a stated period or a lifetime pattern under its selected option.

02

Premium patterns describe the money going in

A single-premium deferred annuity starts with one contribution and postpones income. A flexible-premium deferred design permits contributions that vary within the contract's limits. A scheduled or fixed-installment design instead specifies a payment pattern. Do not confuse a fixed premium schedule with a fixed interest guarantee; one describes funding and the other describes the contract's credited return. David should check minimum payments, whether later contributions are accepted, and whether each new contribution begins its own surrender-charge period. Stopping contributions is not the same event as surrendering the contract. Existing value and available benefits must be evaluated under the issued provisions.

03

Available cash can differ from the account value

Assume David's annuity has a one hundred thousand dollar account value. The question states that surrendering today incurs a five percent charge on the entire amount, with no free allowance, market adjustment or other charge. The charge is five thousand dollars, leaving ninety-five thousand dollars before any tax. This is an insurer charge, not the federal additional tax on certain early distributions. Some contracts permit limited withdrawals without a surrender charge, but the percentage, timing and calculation base vary. An amount described as penalty-free by the insurer can still have tax consequences. Read which cost is waived before promising what David will actually keep.

04

Nonforfeiture does not promise an unrestricted full refund

Nonforfeiture provisions preserve specified benefits when contributions stop or the owner exercises a permitted option. For contracts within Maryland's individual deferred-annuity law, the required provisions address paid-up annuity benefits and applicable surrender values. A paid-up deferred benefit uses the value already built to support a later benefit without further contributions. It is not automatically a refund of every premium on demand. Maryland's minimum calculation uses defined net considerations, interest and adjustments; it is not simply the current advertised rate applied to every deposit. The law also has scope exceptions, including variable and immediate annuities. Apply the correct contract category before importing a minimum-value rule.

05

A bailout clause is a conditional exit right

A bailout provision can allow withdrawal without the stated surrender charge if the insurer's renewal rate falls below a contract-defined level. Suppose the fictional provision sets a three percent threshold and the new credited rate is two and a half percent. The stated rate condition is met, but David must still follow the election window and other terms. A lower rate that remains above the threshold does not satisfy that example. Not every annuity has a bailout clause, and some designs use a rate cap rather than a credited interest rate. A contractual waiver does not erase federal income tax or every possible adjustment unless the applicable rule says so.

06

Death during accumulation requires the actual benefit formula

Before income begins, a deferred contract may provide a death benefit based on account value, adjusted premiums or another stated guarantee. Suppose the contract promises the greater of eighty thousand dollars of eligible adjusted premiums or ninety thousand dollars of current account value. With no other adjustment, the stated benefit is ninety thousand dollars. That is not automatically income-tax-free life insurance proceeds merely because payment follows a death. After annuitization, the selected income option can produce a different outcome for survivors. Review the phase, the triggering person's death and the formula. Funding, surrender and death are separate events, so use the provision that matches the question.

More detail

Classify along separate dimensions

Immediate income begins within one year; deferred income begins later. Single-premium, scheduled-premium and flexible-premium describe contributions. Fixed, indexed and variable describe different value mechanics.

Example: One deposit followed by ten years of accumulation is a single-premium deferred arrangement.

Read each contribution’s charge clock

The cited SEC-filed example applies surrender schedules separately to purchase payments. A contract’s age does not necessarily establish that every recent contribution is beyond all charges. Free-withdrawal allowances can use account value or premiums and may not apply to full surrender.

Example: The lesson’s 5% calculation expressly assumes no allowance or other adjustment.

Maryland’s nonforfeiture law has a defined scope

Insurance §16-501 excludes the listed categories, including variable annuities, immediate annuities and deferred contracts after payments begin. §16-503 requires the applicable paid-up-benefit provisions, surrender provisions when applicable, and information about minimum values and adjustments. A contract lacking specified cash surrender or death benefits must prominently disclose that fact under the provision.

Example: Nonforfeiture does not mean all annuities must offer an unrestricted cash refund.

A statutory minimum is different from an advertised crediting rate

Section 16-504 uses 87.5% of gross considerations as the specified net considerations, a prescribed interest method and deductions for specified withdrawals, charges, premium tax and indebtedness. The current statutory interest floor is 0.15%, subject to the section’s calculation; it is not a universal current credited rate or a guaranteed 3% return on every premium.

Example: Do not use an old 1% statutory floor as the current Maryland rule.

An allowed payment deferral requires more than an insurer’s preference

Where §16-503(c) applies, a contract may reserve up to six months to defer cash surrender payment, but the insurer must request and receive the Commissioner’s written approval, addressing necessity and equitability.

Example: A contractual reservation does not eliminate the required regulatory approval.

Bailout rights are contractual and conditional

General fixed-annuity bailout descriptions use a renewal rate below a specified threshold. Other products may use a cap trigger. The contract controls the election window and what value, charge or adjustment is affected. This differs from a free-look return right.

Example: A surrender-charge waiver alone does not waive federal distribution taxes.

Death-benefit guarantees are not interchangeable

An accumulation-stage death formula can differ from surrender value and from payments continuing after annuitization. Identify the contractual trigger and recipient. Annuity death distributions have tax rules different from ordinary life-insurance death proceeds, addressed in later lessons.

Example: The greater of $80,000 and $90,000 is $90,000 under the stated formula.

Learning objectives

By the end, you can…

  • Classify contracts by income timing and premium pattern.
  • Calculate a stated surrender charge and distinguish it from a tax.
  • Explain paid-up values, bailout conditions and accumulation-stage death benefits.
