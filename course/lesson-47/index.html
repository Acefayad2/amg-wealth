Explanations & examples

01

A product name is not the whole promise

Rosa compares three ways an annuity can develop value. One credits interest declared by the insurer. Another uses selected investment portfolios. A third calculates interest using a market index and a contract formula. The key question is who bears the investment risk and exactly what is guaranteed. Income timing is a different classification: fixed, indexed or variable describes the design, while immediate or deferred describes when income starts. An early withdrawal can also produce a market value adjustment under some contracts. Keep the source of growth, minimum promise, withdrawal rules and selected income benefit separate when comparing these choices.

02

Fixed does not make every future rate identical

A traditional fixed annuity is supported by the insurer's general account. The insurer bears the investment risk behind its contractual guarantees. Suppose Rosa's contract guarantees at least two percent interest but currently credits four percent for the stated period. Four percent is not automatically promised forever. A later declared rate can change within the contract's limits while the minimum guarantee remains applicable. Some designs guarantee a stated rate for several years; read that period. A fixed income option can provide level dollar payments, but level dollars do not guarantee level purchasing power. Inflation can reduce what the same monthly amount buys.

03

Variable value follows the selected investments

David considers a variable annuity. Amounts allocated to its investment subaccounts are held in a separate account, with results tied to the chosen portfolios. He bears the investment risk for that allocation, and value can fall. In a simplified example with no charges or other changes, fifty thousand dollars experiencing a ten percent loss becomes forty-five thousand dollars. Actual fees and withdrawals also affect value. Variable income payments can change with investment results under the payout formula. A guarantee applies only to the benefit and conditions it names. Read its limits instead of assuming all account values are protected. Variable annuities are securities as well as insurance products, with prospectus disclosures and applicable securities requirements.

04

An index credit is calculated, not copied

A fixed indexed annuity uses a specified index to calculate interest under the contract. It does not give Rosa direct ownership of that index's stocks. Suppose the stated method uses an eight percent index increase, a fifty percent participation rate and a three percent cap, with no spread or other adjustment. Half of eight percent is four percent, then the cap limits the credited rate to three percent. Apply the provided order and formula. A zero index-credit floor prevents a negative index credit under that provision; it does not eliminate surrender charges, rider costs or other reductions. Registered index-linked annuities are different and can expose value to index losses.

05

An early exit can change the amount available

A market-value-adjusted annuity may guarantee interest for a specified period while adjusting value if the owner exits at a time covered by its formula. Such designs are also associated with the term modified guaranteed annuity. Generally, an increase in relevant market interest rates can produce a negative adjustment; a decrease can produce a positive adjustment. The actual formula and timing control. A market value adjustment is distinct from a surrender charge, and both may apply. Do not assume keeping the credited interest guarantee means every early withdrawal returns the same amount. Registered market-value-adjustment annuities also have securities disclosures; the presence of an adjustment alone does not replace a product's actual classification.

06

Compare the decision the client is actually making

Rosa wants predictable promises and David is willing to accept investment fluctuations. Their preferences begin the discussion, but neither preference alone establishes the right recommendation. Compare costs, accessible cash, the time horizon, the insurer, the actual guarantees and the income option. A high illustrated return is not a guaranteed result. An indexed design is not automatically a variable annuity, and a separate account is not the same as the insurer's general account. For an exam, identify the design from its mechanics. Then answer the particular question about value, credited interest, investment risk or surrender. A product label cannot override the numbers and conditions explicitly given.

More detail

General account and separate account

Traditional fixed guarantees are general-account obligations. The insurer manages investments supporting its promises. Variable investment subaccounts expose the allocated value to selected portfolio results; any fixed allocation or optional insurance guarantee has its own terms.

Example: A guaranteed death benefit is not automatically a guarantee that today’s variable surrender value cannot decline.

Read the rate period

A guaranteed minimum, a currently declared rate and a rate guaranteed for a specified multi-year period are different promises. A fixed dollar income amount can remain unchanged while inflation reduces purchasing power.

Example: A 4% current rate with a 2% guaranteed minimum does not, by itself, promise 4% in every future year.

Participation, caps and spreads

A participation rate applies a stated proportion of index change. A cap limits the credited result. A spread or margin subtracts a stated amount under its formula. Index measurement may use point-to-point or averaging methods, and index dividends may be excluded. Use the contract’s calculation order and renewal limits.

Example: With an expressly stated 8% index change, 50% participation, 3% cap and no other adjustment, the result is min(8% × 50%, 3%) = 3%.

A floor protects the stated credit, not every transaction

A zero index-credit floor in a fixed indexed annuity means the applicable index-credit calculation does not become negative. Charges, withdrawals, adjustments and insurer performance remain relevant. A registered index-linked annuity (RILA) can impose index-related losses under its buffer or floor arrangement and is a security.

Example: Do not carry a fixed indexed annuity’s zero-credit assumption into a RILA scenario.

Variable payments and guarantees

Investment performance and expenses affect variable accumulation values. Variable income payments follow the elected payout formula and can vary. Optional guarantees must be separately evaluated for fees, eligibility, allocation and withdrawal limits. The product and underlying-fund prospectuses explain risks and charges.

Example: A positive gross portfolio result does not automatically equal the policy’s net result after all expenses.

Market value adjustment is a separate calculation

An MVA reflects specified market-rate changes and transaction timing. In a typical design, higher relevant market rates produce a downward adjustment on an early exit and lower rates can produce an upward adjustment. Product formulas, limits and exceptions control. A surrender charge may apply independently.

Example: If a question expressly states a $2,000 negative adjustment and a separate $3,000 surrender charge on $100,000 with no other effects, available value before tax is $95,000.

Correct the apparent outline duplication

The linked Prometric outline repeats fixed-account-style wording under the variable heading. This course teaches the verified distinction: general-account fixed guarantees versus separate-account variable investment exposure. A publication formatting error is not a reason to teach the two as identical.

Example: Identify the actual investment mechanics when answering a variable annuity question.

Learning objectives

By the end, you can…

  • Separate a guaranteed minimum from a current declared rate.
  • Calculate a stated index credit without claiming direct index ownership.
  • Identify investment risk and market-value adjustments.
