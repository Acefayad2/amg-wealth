Explanations & examples

01

Having a lump sum does not decide where it belongs

Rosa receives a lump sum and asks about retirement income. First identify how much she needs for emergencies, debts and upcoming expenses, as well as the income gap she wants to fill. An annuity can support long-term accumulation or a chosen income promise, but tying up essential near-term cash can conflict with her needs. The producer must understand the product's access rules, costs, guarantees and risks. In a covered Maryland recommendation, the producer's or insurer's financial interest must not come ahead of the consumer's interest. A large commission or attractive opening rate cannot replace a reasonable basis for the recommendation.

02

A retirement account and an annuity are different layers

Annuities can fund eligible individual or group retirement arrangements, including an individual retirement annuity. The account or plan establishes its tax rules, contribution conditions and distribution requirements. The insurance contract supplies its particular benefits and costs. Placing an annuity inside a traditional tax-deferred retirement account does not create a second layer of tax deferral. The recommendation needs a reason grounded in the insurance features and the client's circumstances. Outside such a plan, eligible nonqualified annuity earnings can grow tax-deferred, but the personal premium is generally paid with after-tax money. Tax-deferred means taxation is postponed under applicable rules, not that every eventual payment is tax-free.

03

Education and charitable goals have separate conditions

David wants money for a grandchild's tuition in two years. Naming education as the purpose does not remove surrender charges or make a nonqualified annuity withdrawal tax-free. An education exception associated with an eligible IRA distribution is not a blanket exception for every commercial annuity. Compare access, taxes and alternatives within the producer's authority. A charitable gift annuity is another distinct arrangement: a donor gives money or property in exchange for an eligible organization's annuity promise to the donor or a nominee. Maryland requires the covered organization to obtain a special permit before issuing these agreements. Charitable purpose does not make every payment entirely tax-free.

04

Gather the facts and explain the recommendation

Maryland's standard includes care, disclosure, conflict and documentation obligations. Obtain the consumer profile: financial resources and needs, experience, objectives, time horizon, liquidity, risk tolerance, funding source and tax status. Use reasonable inquiry into options available within your authority, and explain why the recommendation addresses the person's situation over the product's life. Disclose your role, product authority, insurer relationships, compensation sources and the right to request a cash-compensation estimate. Explain material product features and restrictions. Identify and avoid, or reasonably manage and disclose, material conflicts. Choosing the lowest commission alone is not the standard. The actual reasoning must support the client's circumstances.

05

A replacement needs a whole-transaction comparison

David already owns an annuity and is offered a new one with a bonus. Compare the old surrender charge, the new surrender period, lost benefits, increased fees and rider costs, and whether the change substantially benefits him over the product's life. Maryland specifically calls for considering another exchange or replacement within the preceding sixty months. A bonus alone does not settle that comparison. Record the recommendation and its basis at the time of recommendation or sale. If the consumer refuses relevant profile information, document the refusal and consequences with the required signed statement. A signature does not manufacture a sound recommendation from missing facts.

06

Finish with training, records and an honest process

A new producer subject to Maryland's annuity training rule needs the approved one-time four-credit training and knowledge of the insurer's product before making the sale. This exam-preparation lesson is not that approved training. Required transaction records are maintained for seven years after the insurer completes the transaction. Each insurance company must supervise recommendations. A producer must not discourage truthful information, complaints or cooperation with an investigation. If a consumer independently chooses an unrecommended transaction, follow the specific acknowledgment and insurer review rules. The chapter has defined scope exceptions. On the exam, identify the client's need, relevant restrictions and the action the producer must take. Then check whether an exception changes the result.

More detail

Use is not a recommendation

A lump sum can support an immediate income election or a deferred contract, depending on the need. Preserve adequate access to money for near-term expenses and emergencies. Retirement income should be compared with other resources, survivor needs and inflation exposure.

Example: Rosa’s receipt of a lump sum does not establish that the entire sum belongs in a long-surrender-period contract.

Keep the tax arrangement separate from the contract

Eligible individual and group retirement plans can use annuity contracts. An individual retirement annuity is an IRA form. The applicable plan or IRA rules govern contributions, deductions and distributions; the contract governs its insurance benefits. A tax-deferred IRA does not receive extra tax deferral merely from holding an annuity.

Example: A recommendation inside an IRA needs a supportable reason for the insurance features and costs.

Tax deferral is not tax exemption

In an eligible personally owned nonqualified annuity, earnings generally are not taxed as they accumulate. Later taxable distributions remain subject to the applicable ordinary-income rules and possible additional tax. Personal nonqualified premiums generally are not deductible.

Example: Calling growth tax-deferred does not mean a withdrawal of earnings is tax-free.

Education funding needs a separate tax and liquidity analysis

A tuition purpose does not itself waive insurer surrender charges or the nonqualified-annuity early-distribution rules. An eligible higher-education exception for an IRA must not be applied indiscriminately to a personally owned nonqualified annuity. Compare the time horizon and actual arrangement; this outline use is not an endorsement of annuities for college savings.

Example: A distribution needed two years from now can conflict with a much longer surrender schedule.

Charitable gift annuities are donor agreements

Under Maryland Insurance §16-114, covered educational or religious organizations, hospitals and community foundations need a special permit before making donor annuity agreements in Maryland. A permitted organization receives money or property in exchange for its promise of annuity payments to the donor or nominee. Eligibility, reserves and filing conditions apply. Do not portray the arrangement as an ordinary commercial insurer contract or promise a fully deductible gift and fully tax-free payments.

Example: Verify the issuing organization and its permit; charitable status by itself is not the permit.

Consumer profile information

COMAR 31.09.12.03 includes age, annual income, financial situation and needs including debts, financial experience, insurance needs, financial objectives, intended use, time horizon, existing assets and financial products, liquidity needs, liquid net worth, risk tolerance including willingness to accept nonguaranteed elements, funding resources and tax status. These factors are evaluated together.

Example: A large account balance does not necessarily mean enough liquid cash remains after purchase.

Care and its limits

The producer exercises reasonable diligence, care and skill, inquires into options available within the producer’s authority, assesses the whole annuity and selected subaccounts and riders, and communicates the recommendation’s basis. The chapter does not require analyzing every product outside that authority, choosing the lowest compensation or automatically providing ongoing monitoring. It does not itself create a fiduciary relationship; separate arrangements can create additional obligations.

Example: A product’s individual advantage does not excuse ignoring its combined costs and restrictions.

Required disclosure is timely

Before recommendation or sale, prominently disclose the producer’s role, authority, insurer relationships, sources and types of cash and noncash compensation, and the right to request more cash-compensation information. On request, disclose a reasonable estimate, potentially as a range or percentage, and whether it recurs and how often. Explain the applicable product features by the required recommendation or sale timing.

Example: Do not wait until after the purchase to first disclose the required relationship and compensation information.

Conflict and compensation rules are related but distinct

Identify and avoid or reasonably manage and disclose material conflicts, including relevant ownership interests. The chapter’s defined material conflict excludes cash and noncash compensation, which is addressed through separate disclosure and supervision provisions. Insurers must eliminate limited-time sales contests, quotas, bonuses and noncash compensation tied to specific annuities; the rule does not prohibit every lawful commission.

Example: An insurer ownership interest can need conflict treatment beyond simply listing a sales commission.

Replacement review covers more than a new bonus

Consider old surrender charges, new surrender periods, benefits lost, increased fees and charges, substantial consumer benefit over the product’s life, and another exchange or replacement—particularly within 60 months. A positive bonus can be offset by larger costs and lost guarantees.

Example: A replacement 48 months ago falls within the stated 60-month lookback consideration.

Documentation and unrecommended transactions

At recommendation or sale, make a written record of the recommendation and basis. Use the required signed acknowledgment for refusal of profile information and understanding its consequences, and for a consumer decision not based on the producer’s recommendation. Defined no-recommendation situations have specific rules; the insurer’s issuance must still be reasonable under circumstances actually known to it.

Example: Do not treat an acknowledgment as a waiver authorizing a recommendation unsupported by the available facts.

Scope, supervision, training and records

COMAR 31.09.12.02 includes defined exceptions such as certain employer retirement-plan transactions and direct-response sales without a recommendation. An individual IRA is not categorically excluded just because it has IRA tax status. The insurer maintains a supervision system and verifies required training. New producers subject to .08 need the approved four-credit training and product knowledge; the old transition provision is not a universal one-credit substitute. Records under .10 remain for seven years after insurer completion and must preserve attachments and signatures in reproducible form.

Example: Completing this exam-prep course does not represent completion of approved annuity continuing education.

Consumers retain complaint and cooperation rights

A producer or insurer must not dissuade a consumer from truthful responses to an insurer’s confirmation request, filing a complaint, or cooperating with an investigation.

Example: A producer cannot tell David to hide his liquidity needs so the recommendation will pass review.

Learning objectives

By the end, you can…

  • Connect an annuity use to timing, liquidity and the actual tax arrangement.
  • Apply Maryland’s care, disclosure, conflict and documentation duties.
  • Evaluate replacement costs and current training and record requirements.
