Explanations & examples

01

A MEC remains life insurance with different access rules

Marcus wants to pay a large premium into a cash-value life policy. The funding pattern can affect whether it becomes a modified endowment contract, usually called a MEC. A MEC still meets the federal definition of life insurance, but it fails the applicable seven-pay funding test or comes from an exchange of another MEC. Its living distributions receive different tax treatment. MEC status does not, by itself, remove the ordinary income-tax exclusion for a qualifying death benefit. Nor does it automatically require annual tax on all inside growth. Identify the transaction: paying a premium, receiving a withdrawal, taking a loan or receiving a death payment.

02

The seven-pay test compares cumulative amounts

The seven-pay test compares premiums paid with the cumulative amount allowed at each point during the testing period. It is based on the actuarially determined premiums for paid-up future benefits after seven level annual payments. It is not simply the premium Marcus prefers or one-seventh of the death benefit. Suppose the facts give a ten thousand dollar seven-pay annual limit and a twenty thousand dollar cumulative limit at the end of year two. Total counted premiums of twenty-two thousand dollars exceed that stated cumulative limit by two thousand dollars. Unless an applicable correction or other rule changes the facts, the funding test fails. Use the supplied limit rather than inventing an actuarial calculation.

03

Changes can require testing again

Passing the first seven years does not make every later policy change irrelevant. A material change can cause a new testing period with required adjustments. Certain benefit reductions can require recalculation using the lower benefit. Once MEC status applies, merely waiting until year eight or repaying a loan does not automatically remove it. A life policy received in exchange for a MEC is also a MEC under the exchange rule. The law provides specified procedures for timely return of excess premiums and other correction situations, but an ordinary withdrawal is not a universal cure. Ask the insurer to determine status and permitted correction before promising the tax result of a funding change.

04

MEC withdrawals take earnings before basis

Compare two teaching policies, each with seventy thousand dollars of cash value and fifty thousand dollars of adjusted investment. Each therefore has twenty thousand dollars of gain. Assume an ordinary fifteen thousand dollar withdrawal from the non-MEC occurs after the first fifteen policy years, with no special rule or other adjustment. It is a basis recovery under those assumptions. Now apply the same withdrawal to the MEC. Its earnings-first rule makes the fifteen thousand dollars taxable ordinary income because twenty thousand dollars of gain is available. If the MEC withdrawal instead totals twenty-five thousand dollars, the first twenty thousand is gain and the remaining five thousand recovers basis.

05

Calling it a loan does not avoid MEC treatment

A MEC loan generally is treated as a distribution for federal tax purposes. Using the same seventy thousand dollar value and fifty thousand dollar adjusted investment, a fifteen thousand dollar loan is taxable to the extent of the available gain under the stated assumptions. Assigning or pledging value can also trigger distribution treatment, subject to specific exceptions. This differs from an ordinary loan under an in-force non-MEC policy. The tax classification does not eliminate the actual loan balance, interest or effect on insurance benefits. Keep those contract consequences separate. Also track prior taxable amounts so a later distribution calculation does not simply tax the same amount twice.

06

The additional tax applies to the taxable portion

Assume Marcus is forty-five and receives a MEC distribution with fifteen thousand dollars of taxable gain, with no applicable exception. The additional federal tax is ten percent of that taxable amount, or fifteen hundred dollars, on top of the ordinary income tax. It is not ten percent of the entire policy value. The additional-tax rule has exceptions, including distributions at age fifty-nine and one-half or later, qualifying disability and qualifying substantially equal periodic payments. An exception to the additional tax does not automatically remove ordinary income tax on the gain. On an exam, establish MEC status, calculate the gain portion, then check age and the actual exception before calculating the extra tax.

More detail

Definition and scope

Section 7702A covers a qualifying life contract entered into on or after June 21, 1988 that fails the seven-pay test, and a life contract received in exchange for a MEC. Older-contract changes have special transition rules. The central distinction is living-distribution taxation; MEC status alone does not cancel life insurance or make all death proceeds taxable.

Example: A contract’s premium schedule title alone is not the actuarial test result.

Cumulative testing

At any point in the applicable testing period, compare the amount counted as paid with the cumulative allowed net level premiums under the statute. This hypothetical actuarial measure differs from the policy’s chosen payment schedule, death benefit and illustrated return. A given cumulative limit must be applied at the stated time.

Example: At a point with a $20,000 cumulative limit, $22,000 counted premiums exceed the limit by $2,000.

Benefit reductions and material changes

A reduction during the first seven contract years generally requires testing as if the policy had originally had the reduced benefits, with specified exceptions. A material change not reflected in a prior determination can treat the contract as new for testing, with cash-value adjustments. Benefit increases have statutory inclusions and exceptions.

Example: Do not assume that a policy older than seven years can never need MEC retesting.

Corrections require the actual legal procedure

Section 7702A(e)(1)(B) provides a defined treatment when an insurer returns premium with interest to comply with the test within 60 days after the end of the relevant contract year. Returned interest is included in income. Other insurer correction procedures have their own requirements. Ordinary withdrawal, loan repayment or waiting is not a general promise to erase MEC status.

Example: The deadline is tied to the end of the contract year, not automatically 60 days after the original payment.

Earnings first and loans

MEC distributions use income-first treatment. Gain is determined using the applicable cash value and adjusted investment; the allocation rule determines cash value without subtracting surrender charges. Loans and pledges generally are deemed distributions, with specific exceptions such as a qualifying limited burial-contract assignment. Prior taxable loan treatment affects investment calculations to avoid simple double counting.

Example: With $20,000 gain available, a $15,000 MEC distribution is entirely gain; a $25,000 distribution includes $20,000 gain and $5,000 basis under the stated assumptions.

Compare with the ordinary non-MEC rule carefully

An ordinary withdrawal after the first 15 policy years can recover basis before income, assuming no other special rule. Certain earlier benefit reductions can produce different results. An in-force non-MEC loan generally is indebtedness, but later lapse or surrender can cause taxable income.

Example: The comparison example expressly supplies policy age and excludes other adjustments; do not apply it to every possible withdrawal.

Additional tax and exceptions

Section 72(v) generally adds 10% of the taxable portion of a MEC distribution. Exceptions include age 59½ or later, statutory disability and qualifying substantially equal periodic payments over the specified life or lives. The extra-tax exception and ordinary gain inclusion are separate determinations.

Example: At age 45 with $15,000 taxable gain and no exception, the additional tax is $1,500, not $7,000 on the full $70,000 policy value.

Status can affect related distributions

The statute addresses distributions in the year the failure takes effect and later years, as well as distributions within two years before failure treated as anticipating it. Section 72 also aggregates MECs issued by the same company to the same policyholder during the same calendar year for the applicable income calculation.

Example: Splitting one funding strategy across same-year contracts does not automatically avoid the tax rules.

Learning objectives

By the end, you can…

  • Apply an expressly stated cumulative seven-pay limit.
  • Compare non-MEC withdrawal treatment with a MEC withdrawal or loan.
  • Calculate the possible additional tax only on the taxable portion.
