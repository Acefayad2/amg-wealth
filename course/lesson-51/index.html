Explanations & examples

01

Identify the contract and the payment

Rosa bought a deferred annuity in her own name with money on which she already paid income tax. It is outside an individual retirement account or employer retirement plan. This is a nonqualified annuity for our examples. Its growth generally can accumulate without annual income tax while held by an individual, but access to the money can create taxable income. Before calculating anything, identify the owner, the contract's issue date and whether the payment is a withdrawal, full surrender or annuity income. Different rules apply to different events. A personal annuity does not become an individual retirement account merely because Rosa intends to use it during retirement.

02

A partial withdrawal normally reaches earnings first

Rosa's teaching contract was issued in twenty twenty, has ninety thousand dollars of cash value and sixty thousand dollars of adjusted investment in the contract. There are no prior distributions or special adjustments. The difference is thirty thousand dollars of gain. Before the annuity starting date, an ordinary partial withdrawal from this contract generally takes taxable earnings before recovering investment. A twenty thousand dollar withdrawal is therefore entirely taxable ordinary income because thirty thousand dollars of gain is available. Do not divide the withdrawal in proportion to premiums and growth. Also do not copy the ordinary basis-first withdrawal treatment from a non-modified life insurance policy.

03

Only the amount beyond available gain recovers basis

Now return to the original ninety thousand dollar value and sixty thousand dollar investment, without making the earlier withdrawal. This time Rosa withdraws forty thousand dollars. The first thirty thousand dollars uses the available gain and is taxable. The remaining ten thousand dollars recovers investment without being taxed again. Her remaining investment is fifty thousand dollars under these simplified facts. The withdrawal is not forty thousand dollars of taxable gain, because the contract contained only thirty thousand dollars of gain. Nor is the entire payment a tax-free return of premiums. Establish the value, adjusted investment and actual transaction amount before choosing the taxable portion.

04

Full surrender uses the actual surrender transaction

A full surrender ends the contract and returns its remaining surrender proceeds. Suppose the insurer states that Rosa would receive eighty-five thousand dollars after all applicable surrender charges, and her unrecovered investment is sixty thousand dollars. Assume no debt or other tax adjustment. The resulting taxable gain is twenty-five thousand dollars. That calculation uses the stated net proceeds for this full surrender. It should not be confused with the partial-withdrawal rule, where gain generally uses cash value before surrender charges. Read whether the question describes a partial payment or complete termination. The same displayed account value does not make those two transactions identical.

05

A waived surrender charge does not waive federal tax

Change the owner to a forty-five-year-old with a twenty thousand dollar taxable withdrawal and no applicable exception. The additional federal tax on an early nonqualified annuity distribution is generally ten percent of the taxable portion. In this example that is two thousand dollars, in addition to ordinary income tax. An insurer's free-withdrawal allowance concerns its contract charge and does not automatically remove either federal tax. Exceptions to the additional tax include distributions after age fifty-nine and one half, qualifying disability, death and certain substantially equal periodic payments. Use the exception that actually applies. An education or first-home exception associated with an individual retirement account is not a blanket commercial-annuity exception.

06

A loan label does not settle the tax question

For a nonqualified annuity, taking a loan or pledging value generally can be treated as receiving a distribution under the applicable tax rule. Do not assume that the familiar loan treatment of an ordinary life policy applies to every insurance contract. The amount treated as distributed must still be evaluated using the correct gain and basis rules. Rosa should obtain the insurer's transaction details before deciding what to do. On the exam, follow a consistent sequence: identify the nonqualified contract, distinguish withdrawal from income or full surrender, calculate the taxable amount, and then consider any additional tax. Keeping those steps separate prevents several common answer traps.

More detail

These examples deliberately use a post-1982 contract

The earnings-first examples assume an individually owned nonqualified annuity issued in 2020, before its annuity starting date, with no prior distributions, aggregation or other adjustment. Certain amounts attributable to pre-August 14, 1982 investment have different ordering. Do not import the simplified example into every historic contract.

Example: A partial withdrawal generally measures inside gain using cash value without a surrender-charge reduction.

Investment is an adjusted record

Premiums already taxed form investment, but prior tax-free recoveries and other required adjustments change the remaining amount. Taxable earnings are ordinary income; inside tax deferral is not a permanent exclusion.

Example: The independent $40,000 example uses $30,000 gain and $10,000 basis, leaving $50,000 investment.

Tax exceptions are account-specific

Section 72(q), rather than an IRA exception list, governs the additional tax in these nonqualified examples. Reaching 59½ can remove this additional tax without excluding otherwise taxable earnings. The section also contains specified exceptions beyond those introduced here.

Example: A contract-charge waiver and a federal exception answer different questions.

Learning objectives

By the end, you can…

  • Calculate earnings-first withdrawals under stated assumptions.
  • Distinguish partial withdrawal from full surrender.
  • Apply an additional-tax rate to the taxable portion only.
