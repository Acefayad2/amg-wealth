Explanations & examples

01

Start with obligations and available resources

Marcus and Elena return for a full family review. In this new exercise, their stated survivor income shortfall is thirty-five thousand dollars per year for fifteen years. Assume no inflation or investment return. That creates five hundred twenty-five thousand dollars of income need. They also identify two hundred thousand dollars of separate immediate obligations, with no overlap between the categories. The total is seven hundred twenty-five thousand dollars. They have one hundred twenty-five thousand dollars of resources explicitly available for these needs. Subtracting those resources leaves a six hundred thousand dollar illustrative gap. This is not a quote or a personal recommendation.

02

Match the duration without promising every design does the same job

The family expects a large income need while the children are dependent, but also wants to understand smaller lasting obligations. A level term policy can provide a stated death benefit during its specified term. Coverage after that period depends on the actual renewal or conversion rights, deadlines and premiums. Whole life uses a different structure, with permanent protection and contractual cash values when its required conditions are maintained. Compare the actual benefits, guarantees, funding and affordable duration. If existing coverage might be replaced, follow Maryland's replacement process and assess lost benefits and costs before asking the family to end that policy.

03

Name each role before discussing the benefit

In the proposed arrangement, Marcus owns a policy on his own life, and Elena is the primary beneficiary. Marcus holds the ownership rights and is also the insured person, but those are two separate roles. Elena's beneficiary designation does not make her life insured by that base policy. A contingent beneficiary is considered when the primary cannot take the benefit under the applicable contract and legal facts. The family also wants funds available for their minor children. Naming a child does not automatically authorize unrestricted direct payment to the child. A properly arranged trust or other lawful method requires attention to the actual designation and legal requirements.

04

An application is not a promise of immediate coverage

Marcus completes the application and gives the initial premium to the producer. Those acts do not by themselves prove that every claim is covered immediately. Read the receipt and its actual coverage conditions, the insurer's underwriting decision, and any delivery requirements. The producer must not invent authority to waive a condition or alter a contract promise. Application answers should be complete and truthful, and corrections should be handled properly. If Marcus's health changes before delivery, follow the insurer's required process instead of hiding that change. A good-health statement can be part of the delivery requirements. The effective-date question is answered by the relevant documents and facts, not by assuming that a handshake created unconditional protection.

05

Keep policy time limits and continuing rights distinct

Later, a premium is missed. Before describing the policy as lapsed, identify the due date and the applicable grace period. For standard Maryland individual life coverage within the cited rule, the grace provision is one month of at least thirty days, with the policy continuing during that period. The contract may provide a longer period. That is different from a free-look period after delivery, a reinstatement opportunity after lapse or an incontestability period. If a covered death occurs during grace, the unpaid premium may be deducted as allowed. Do not tell the family that every missed payment ends protection immediately, or that reinstatement is automatic without meeting its requirements. Trace the dates and the particular provision before deciding.

06

Calculate the stated benefit and preserve the claim record

Finish with a separate claim example. The issued policy has a one hundred thousand dollar death benefit, a twelve thousand dollar outstanding loan and six hundred dollars of accrued loan interest. Assume a covered death, no other benefit additions and no other deductions. The net payment is eighty-seven thousand four hundred dollars. Do not add the cash value to the face amount unless the actual contract provides that result. The agent should help the beneficiary follow the insurer's claim process and keep clear records, while the insurer determines the payment under the contract. Good exam answers follow the stated person, event, provision and calculation.

More detail

The needs example deliberately uses simplified assumptions

The workshop assumes a $35,000 annual shortfall for 15 years with zero interest and inflation, $200,000 of separate immediate obligations and $125,000 of genuinely available resources. Do not count mortgage payments again after separately funding a mortgage payoff.

Example: The result is an illustrative $600,000 gap, not an individualized recommendation.

An insured person is not the same as a beneficiary

A spouse named to receive proceeds is not thereby insured by the base coverage. A spouse rider, a first-death joint contract and survivorship coverage have different claim triggers. A minor beneficiary can require an appropriate legal payment arrangement.

Example: Read the named lives, benefit schedule and beneficiary succession instead of adding every household amount together.

Use the exact Maryland grace-period scope

Insurance §16-202 addresses standard individual life policies in its applicable scope: a grace period of one month, not less than 30 days, for premiums after the first, with continued coverage during the period. Industrial and other separately governed contracts require their own rule.

Example: Do not replace the statutory wording with a claim that every life policy universally has exactly 31 days.

Learning objectives

By the end, you can…

  • Calculate an explicitly stated protection gap.
  • Separate policy roles, product duration and delivery conditions.
  • Apply a claim calculation without mixing benefit amounts.
